\section{Why Cambodia Needs a Daily Price Tracker}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Cambodia Uses Two Currencies at the Same Time}
Cambodia is unique because people use two currencies every day: the United States Dollar (USD) and the Cambodian Riel (KHR).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Where Dollars are used:} In modern supermarkets (like AEON and Lucky), phone shops, electronics stores, restaurant chains, and online shopping apps, prices are almost always shown in US Dollars.
    \item \textbf{Where Riel is used:} In traditional open markets, street food stalls, local tuk-tuks, and for government utility bills (like your home electricity and clean tap water), prices are quoted in Cambodian Riel.
\end{itemize}
Because both currencies are used side by side, when the exchange rate moves, prices in shops can change quickly. For example, if a family earns their living in Riel, but buys groceries in a supermarket priced in Dollars, any drop in the value of the Riel means their groceries immediately cost more money.

\subsection{What Cambodian Families Spend Most of Their Money On}
According to official household surveys from the government's National Institute of Statistics (NIS), Cambodian families spend almost all of their monthly budget on three basic things:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Food and Non-Alcoholic Drinks (44.8\%):} Nearly half of every dollar spent goes directly to eating and drinking (rice, pork, fish, cooking oil, and vegetables). For poorer families, food can take more than 55\% of their budget!
    \item \textbf{Housing, Water, Electricity, and Gas (17.1\%):} Paying for rent, home power, cooking gas, and water.
    \item \textbf{Transport and Fuel (12.2\%):} Gasoline for motorbikes, diesel for trucks, and bus tickets.
\end{enumerate}
Together, these three categories make up \textbf{74\% of all household spending}. That means when global fuel prices go up, or when meat and rice prices rise, regular families feel the squeeze immediately.

\subsection{Why the Traditional Monthly Survey is Too Slow}
In the past, the only way to measure inflation was through official monthly surveys. Government workers would walk into physical markets with clipboards once a month, write down prices by hand, and take them back to the office to calculate.

While this traditional method is thorough, it has three big problems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{It is 3 to 4 weeks late:} The official report for January is usually not published until late February. If food or gasoline prices jump today, leaders at the Central Bank and government ministries do not see the official numbers until a month later.
    \item \textbf{It only checks prices once a month:} If inspectors visit a store on the 10th day of the month, they completely miss sales, discounts, or price jumps that happen on the 20th or 28th.
    \item \textbf{It takes a lot of time and people:} Walking to hundreds of stalls in heavy traffic is slow, expensive, and easy to make mistakes when copying numbers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Our Daily Solution: Automated Web Intelligence}
Our automated system solves this problem. Every single morning at 2:00 AM, while the city is asleep, our software automatically visits 25 online store catalogs, fuel company websites, utility regulators, phone providers, vehicle retailers, and bus portals. It collects over 35,500 real prices every day, cleans them, and calculates today's true inflation rate. Government leaders and researchers can see price changes right now, rather than waiting a whole month.

\section{The Data Pipeline Architecture (The Medallion Journey)}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The 3 Refining Steps (The Medallion Approach)}
Think of data like metal dug out of the ground. When it first comes out, it is raw and dirty. It needs to be cleaned, melted down, and turned into something pure and useful. We organize our data into 3 simple stages:

\begin{center}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white,colframe=NavyBlue,width=0.95\textwidth,title=\bfseries The 3 Steps: Raw Data to Final Numbers]
\small
\textbf{Step 1: BRONZE (Raw Store Prices)}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item We collect raw text and price tags from 23 store websites every morning at 2:00 AM.
    \item Exactly as the stores wrote them, typos and all. Nothing is thrown away.
    \item Stored in our raw database table: \texttt{bronze.raw\_prices}.
\end{itemize}

\centering $\Downarrow$ \textit{Clean up text, convert Dollars to Riel, match names, remove errors} \\
\raggedright

\textbf{Step 2: SILVER (Clean, Standardized Items)}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Fix spelling and standardize sizes (make sure $1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg}$ and $500\text{ml} = 0.5\text{L}$).
    \item Convert all US Dollar prices into Cambodian Riel using today's official exchange rate.
    \item Connect Khmer names and English names so the computer knows they are the same product.
    \item Sort every item into its correct official category (Food, Medicine, Tech, Transport).
    \item Remove crazy errors (like an item marked down by 99\% by mistake).
\end{itemize}

\centering $\Downarrow$ \textit{Run fair price math, fill in out-of-stock items, calculate inflation} \\
\raggedright

\textbf{Step 3: GOLD (Final Daily Inflation Reports)}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Calculate the average price change for each group of items using the fair Jevons rule.
    \item Fill in missing prices if a product is temporarily out of stock.
    \item Weight each group by how much real families spend on it (Food gets 44.8\%, Transport gets 12.2\%).
    \item Produce the final numbers: National Daily CPI, Core CPI (without food and gas), and a prediction of where the month will finish.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

\subsection{Detailed Layer-by-Layer Workflows}
To ensure the system is a perfect digital twin of the code, we follow these exact operational workflows every single day.

\subsubsection{The Bronze Workflow: Raw Ingestion}
The goal of the Bronze layer is to capture the reality of the market without any filtering.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Trigger:} Apache Airflow wakes up the \texttt{cpi\_master\_dag} at 2:00 AM.
    \item \textbf{Scrape:} 23 independent scrapers visit store APIs and web pages using \texttt{curl\_cffi} to bypass security.
    \item \textbf{Ingest:} Raw text, raw prices, and timestamps are upserted into \texttt{bronze.raw\_prices}.
    \item \textbf{Telemetry:} The system records the number of rows collected per store in \texttt{staging.bronze\_ingestion\_stats} to detect if a store website is down.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{The Silver Workflow: The Intelligence Engine}
The Silver layer turns messy text into a standardized product catalog.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Text Cleaning:} Strip promotional noise (\textit{"Special Offer!"}) and normalize units ($1000\text{ml} \to 1\text{L}$).
    \item \textbf{Currency Standardization:} Convert all USD prices to KHR using the NBC morning exchange rate.
    \item \textbf{Item Matching:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Generate a 768-dimensional AI vector for the product name.
        \item Search the catalog for the closest match using Cosine Similarity.
        \item Apply \textbf{Spec Guardrails} (Storage, Pack size, Volume) to prevent false merges.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Taxonomy Assignment (The AI Ladder):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Tier 1:} Store Purity (e.g., Pharmacy $\to$ Health).
        \item \textbf{Tier 2:} Semantic Vector Search ($\text{Similarity} \ge 0.85$).
        \item \textbf{Tier 3:} Local AI (Llama 3.1 Classifier $\to$ Judge).
        \item \textbf{Tier 4:} Cloud Arbitration (Gemini Pro).
        \item \textbf{Tier 5:} Human Expert Override.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Final Store:} Standardized records are saved to \texttt{silver.clean\_store\_prices}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{The Gold Workflow: The Econometric Engine}
The Gold layer transforms individual price tags into national economic indicators.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Elementary Indexing:} Calculate price ratios against the base period and combine them using the \textbf{Jevons Geometric Mean}.
    \item \textbf{Gap Imputation:} If a product is out of stock, estimate its price using the average movement of its category.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Aggregation:} Combine indices from Item $\to$ Subclass $\to$ Group $\to$ Division $\to$ National Headline CPI using Laspeyres weights.
    \item \textbf{Nowcasting:} Project the remaining days of the month by analyzing 7-day momentum in Food and Transport, FX pass-through drift, and festive shocks.
    \item \textbf{Splicing:} Link the pipeline's 100-base index to the official National Institute of Statistics (NIS) 2006-base index.
    \item \textbf{Reporting:} Final results are published to \texttt{gold.fct\_cpi\_daily} and \texttt{gold.fct\_cpi\_nowcast}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{How Airflow and Cosmos Keep Everything Running on Time}
To make sure this entire process happens smoothly without humans needing to press buttons at 2:00 AM, we use an automatic manager called \textbf{Apache Airflow} and a tool called \textbf{Astronomer Cosmos}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Master Alarm Clock (\texttt{cpi\_master\_dag}):} Rings every day at 2:00 AM. It starts all 23 store scrapers at the same time. Once they finish and at least 3 sources report good data, it wakes up the next step.
    \item \textbf{The Cleaning Step (\texttt{silver\_dag}):} Runs our data cleaning models. Thanks to Cosmos, each cleaning task is its own step. If one step has an issue, we can see exactly which one it is, while the rest keep going.
    \item \textbf{The Final Calculation Step (\texttt{gold\_cpi\_dag}):} Calculates today's official daily inflation rate and updates our dashboards before breakfast.
    \item \textbf{The Weekly Housekeeping Step (\texttt{cpi\_maintenance\_dag}):} Runs every Sunday at 1:00 AM to organize database folders for the coming months and keep the system fast.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Splitting Big Tables into Monthly Folders}
In one year, we collect almost 13 million price records! If you dump 13 million rows into one giant table, searching for today's price is like looking for a needle in a massive haystack. It would take several seconds for every click.

Instead, we use \textbf{Declarative Range Partitioning}. Think of this as putting each month's receipts into its own labeled physical folder (like \texttt{prices\_2026\_08} and \texttt{prices\_2026\_09}). Our system is proactive: every week, it automatically creates these folders 3 months into the future, so there is never a gap in collection. When someone asks: \textit{"What is today's price?"}, the computer ignores all the old folders and only looks inside today's folder. The answer comes back in less than \textbf{8 milliseconds}!

\subsection{Visiting Websites Politely Without Getting Blocked}
Big e-commerce websites deploy automated defenses (such as Cloudflare or AWS Shield) to protect their servers. Here is how our scrapers operate responsibly and ensure 100\% daily collection success:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Impersonating Standard Web Browsers:} Standard Python scripts look robotic to security filters. We use advanced browser emulation (\texttt{curl\_cffi}) that matches the exact cryptographic TLS handshake (JA3/JA4 fingerprint) of Google Chrome on Windows.
    \item \textbf{Polite Pauses and Randomized Jitter:} We never hammer a website with dozens of requests at once. Our scrapers include randomized pauses (1 to 2 seconds between pages) so we never add noticeable load to store servers.
    \item \textbf{Using Mobile and Public API Endpoints:} Whenever possible, we query the lightweight JSON APIs used by mobile apps rather than downloading heavy web pages with advertising and tracking scripts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Never Recording the Same Price Twice (Deduplication)}
Sometimes an internet connection drops or a task needs to retry. What happens if our scraper runs twice on the same morning? Does it record double the prices?

No. We enforce a strict unique database rule: each item at each store can only have \textbf{one price per calendar day}. If the scraper runs a second time on the same day, it uses an \texttt{ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE} command, refreshing today's price in-place rather than creating duplicate records. This guarantees that our daily inflation calculations remain clean, honest, and mathematically sound.

\section{Deep Dive: Cleaning and Classifying (Silver Layer)}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The Khmer and English Language Challenge}
In Cambodia, retail shelves are multilingual. The exact same bottle of milk might be listed in Khmer script in one store, in English in another store, or mixed together:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Store A: \textit{"\khmer{ទឹកដោះគោស្រស់} 1L"} (Khmer)
    \item Store B: \textit{"Fresh Milk 1000ml"} (English)
    \item Store C: \textit{"Meiji Fresh Milk \khmer{ទឹកដោះគោ} 1 Litre"} (Mixed)
\end{itemize}
If a computer just looks at the letters, it thinks these are three completely different products! Our Silver cleaning layer translates common terms, normalizes units ($1000\text{ml} = 1\text{L}$, $1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg}$), and cleans the text so the computer understands that they represent the exact same product.

\subsection{Text Cleaning and Unit Normalization Rules}
Before any vector matching takes place, raw text goes through deterministic cleaning in \texttt{pipeline/text\_clean.py}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strip Promotional Jargon:} Removes marketing noise such as \textit{"Special Offer!"}, \textit{"Buy 1 Get 1 Free"}, \textit{"Hot Promo"}, and \textit{"New Arrival"}.
    \item \textbf{Harmonize Measurement Units:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Mass: Grams, gm, and g are converted to standard kilograms (\text{kg}).
        \item Volume: Milliliters (ml), ltr, and liter are converted to standard liters (\text{L}).
        \item Packaging: Single items, 6-packs, and cases of 24 cans are extracted into explicit item quantities.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dual Currency Conversion:} Converts all USD prices to Cambodian Riel using the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) official morning exchange rate:
    $$P_{\text{KHR}} = P_{\text{USD}} \times \text{FX}_{\text{NBC}}$$
    This price in Riel is permanently saved, ensuring that later calculations never accidentally convert it twice.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{AI Vector Embeddings: Matching Items to Catalog}
Every morning, scrapers collect thousands of product listings. To track an item's price over time, we must match today's scraped name against our master catalog of over 40,000 known canonical products (\texttt{silver.dim\_canonical\_items}).

\subsubsection{The Mathematical Cosine Similarity}
To compare the scraped item vector $\vec{u}$ and a catalog item vector $\vec{v}$, we calculate their \textbf{cosine similarity}:
\begin{equation}
\text{Similarity}(\vec{u}, \vec{v}) = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\| \|\vec{v}\|} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{768} u_i v_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{768} u_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{768} v_i^2}}
\end{equation}
The resulting score ranges from $0.0$ (totally unrelated) to $1.0$ (identical in meaning).

\subsubsection{The 3 Matching Tiers and Decision Rules}
In \texttt{pipeline/vector\_item\_matcher.py}, the system applies strict decision boundaries:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1: High Confidence Match ($\text{Similarity} \ge 0.88$)} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{\texttt{APPROVE\_MATCH}}
    \item \textbf{Tier 2: Borderline Similarity ($0.75 \le \text{Similarity} < 0.88$)} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{AI Arbitration} (Gemini Flash judges if the items are the same).
    \item \textbf{Tier 3: Low Similarity ($\text{Similarity} < 0.75$)} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{\texttt{SPLIT\_NEW}} (creates a new product in the catalog).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Crucial Spec Guardrails (Preventing False Merges)}
Vector embeddings can be tricked by numbers. \textit{"iPhone 15 128GB"} and \textit{"iPhone 15 512GB"} look identical to AI but have very different prices. To stop this, \texttt{vector\_item\_matcher.py} enforces 4 deterministic guardrails:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Storage Guard:} Rejects matches if electronic memory differs (e.g. 128GB vs 256GB).
    \item \textbf{Pack Quantity Guard:} Rejects matches if pack sizes differ (e.g. 1 single can vs a 24-can case).
    \item \textbf{Volume/Mass Guard:} Rejects matches if volume or weight differs by more than 10\%.
    \item \textbf{Flavor Variant Guard:} Rejects matching Diet / Zero Sugar drinks with regular sugary drinks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Local-First AI Classification Architecture}
Every product must be assigned to one of the official UN COICOP categories. To do this accurately and cheaply, we use a \textbf{Local-First Intelligence Architecture} (the Classification Ladder):

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1: Verified Cache Lookup:} Direct check of \texttt{silver.classification\_cache}. Stale entries marked \texttt{UNCLASSIFIED}, \texttt{99}, or \texttt{REVIEW} are actively bypassed to allow deterministic rules to evaluate.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2: Deterministic Rules \& Store Purity Locks:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Store Purity Locks:} Realestate/Khmer24 $\to$ Housing (04), PPWSA/EDC $\to$ Utilities (04), Bus portals $\to$ Transport (07), Community Pharma $\to$ Health (06).
        \item \textbf{Critical Traps:} Evaluated with PostgreSQL-compliant word boundary regex (\texttt{\textbackslash ypattern\textbackslash y}). For example, Panasonic hair dryers are deterministically mapped to Personal Care (12.1.1) rather than generic appliances (05). Incremental runs dynamically re-evaluate items matching updated traps.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 3: Hierarchical AI Drill-Down (Llama 3.1 \& Gemini Judge):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{The Classifier:} Sequentially navigates Division $\to$ Group $\to$ Class $\to$ Subclass.
        \item \textbf{The Judge:} Audits the proposal against COICOP standards. Approved if confidence $\ge 80\%$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 4: Deterministic Fallbacks:} Global overrides, category maps, and store defaults.
    \item \textbf{Tier 5: Human Expert Review:} The final safety net for unclassified or borderline items.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white,colframe=Teal,title=\textbf{Step-by-Step Example: Gemini AI Classifying Instant Noodles}]
\textbf{1. Input Product:} \textit{"Indomie Mi Goreng Fried Noodles Cup 75g"} (Store: AEON, Category: \textit{"Grocery > Instant Noodles"}, Price: 3,200 KHR)\\
\textbf{2. Resolution Path:} Tiers 1--2 fail. The pipeline invokes the AI engine.\\
\textbf{3. Multi-Attribute Prompt:} Gemini receives the name, store, category, and price.\\
\textbf{4. Structured Classification:} Gemini returns: \texttt{\{"coicop\_code": "01.1.1", "confidence\_score": 0.99, "reasoning": "Edible cereal-based food product..."\}}\\
\textbf{5. Cache Storage:} The result is saved in \texttt{silver.dim\_coicop\_ai\_cache} for instant resolution in future scrapes.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{The Econometric Engine (Gold Layer)}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The Rules of Fair Price Math}
To avoid the \textbf{Carli Bias} (where simple averages create fake inflation), we use the \textbf{Jevons Geometric Mean}. If an egg goes from \$1 to \$2 (+100\%) and back to \$1 (-50\%), the simple average says +25\% inflation, while Jevons correctly says \textbf{0\% change}.

\subsection{Handling Out-of-Stock Items and Product Upgrades}
\subsubsection{Out-of-Stock (Class-Mean Imputation)}
If a product is missing today, we don't use \$0.00. Instead, we look at the average movement of its category (e.g., if other oils rose 1\%, we assume the missing oil rose 1\%). If an item is missing for \textbf{7 consecutive days}, it is officially retired from the basket.

\subsubsection{Product Upgrades (Hedonic Regression Dual-Engine)}
When a new electronics model is released at a higher price, we separate the cost of \textit{better features} (RAM, storage, camera) from \textit{pure inflation} using \textbf{Log-Linear Hedonic Quality Adjustment} (\texttt{pipeline/hedonic\_regression.py}).
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Primary Solver:} Uses statsmodels OLS when installed.
    \item \textbf{Analytical NumPy Fallback:} Automatically falls back to Ordinary Least Squares via \texttt{numpy.linalg.lstsq} with identical formulation, ensuring quality adjustments succeed even in lightweight worker environments.
    \item \textbf{Non-Blocking Execution:} In Airflow, unexpected model skips raise an \texttt{AirflowSkipException}, allowing the pipeline to succeed without blocking downstream CPI calculations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hierarchical Aggregation: From Item to National CPI}
A national CPI is a pyramid. We combine data from the bottom up:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Level 1 (Items):} Collect daily prices for thousands of products.
    \item \textbf{Level 2 (Subclasses):} Combine items into groups like \textit{"Bread and Cereals"} using the Jevons Mean.
    \item \textbf{Level 3 (Groups):} Combine subclasses into larger groups using expenditure weights.
    \item \textbf{Level 4 (Divisions):} Combine groups into the 12 Major Divisions.
    \item \textbf{Level 5 (National Headline CPI):} The final weighted sum of all 12 divisions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Predicting the Rest of the Month (Nowcasting)}
Official reports are 4 weeks late. Our \texttt{ml/nowcaster.py} engine predicts the monthly outcome in real-time using 10 mathematical equations.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Momentum:} It checks the 7-day price velocity of Food and Transport.
    \item \textbf{FX Drift:} It calculates how much the USD/KHR exchange rate is pushing prices up.
    \item \textbf{Festive Shocks:} It adds known demand surges for Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben.
    \item \textbf{Blended Result:} It combines observed days with the projected remaining days to give a \textbf{Nowcast CPI} with a 95\% Confidence Interval.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Mathematical Foundation (The 24 Formulas)}
% =============================================================================

Every mathematical equation used in the pipeline has a clear purpose. Here is each formula explained in plain English.

\subsection{Core Indexing Formulas}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eq 1: Base Price ($P_{i, 0}$):} The geometric average price over the base period.
    \item \textbf{Eq 2: Price Ratio ($R_{i, t}$):} Today's price divided by the base price.
    \item \textbf{Eq 3: Outlier Bound:} Blocks prices that jump more than $5\times$ (prevents typos from ruining the index).
    \item \textbf{Eq 5: Imputation ($\widehat{P}_{i, t}$):} Estimates missing prices using group momentum.
    \item \textbf{Eq 6: Jevons Index ($I_{J, c}$):} The fair average price change for a group of items.
    \item \textbf{Eq 8: National Headline CPI:} The final weighted sum of all 12 divisions.
    \item \textbf{Eq 9: Core CPI:} The inflation rate excluding Food, Utilities, and Transport.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{AI and Quality Formulas}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eq 11: Cosine Similarity:} Measures the semantic meaning between two product titles.
    \item \textbf{Eq 12: Token-Sort Fuzzy Similarity:} Handles different word orders (e.g., \textit{"Beer Angkor"} vs \textit{"Angkor Beer"}).
    \item \textbf{Eq 14: Hedonic Regression:} Measures how much extra shoppers pay for better hardware (RAM, Storage).
    \item \textbf{Eq 15: Constant-Quality Price:} Subtracts the value of tech upgrades to isolate pure inflation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Nowcasting Formulas}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eq 16: Leading Drift ($\hat{\delta}_{\text{leading}}$):} Combines trailing 7-day momentum from Food and Transport.
    \item \textbf{Eq 17: FX Drift ($\hat{\delta}_{\text{FX}}$):} Measures inflation caused by the USD/KHR exchange rate.
    \item \textbf{Eq 18: Festive Shock ($\phi_{\text{fest}}$):} Adds temporary price spikes during national holidays.
    \item \textbf{Eq 21: Blended Nowcast:} Combines observed days with projected remaining days.
    \item \textbf{Eq 24: NIS Splicing:} Connects the pipeline result to the official National Institute of Statistics (NIS) 2006-base index.
\end{itemize}

\section{Macroeconomic Integration: Currency and History}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The Dual-Currency Impact}
Because Cambodia uses both currencies, the exchange rate directly changes prices.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supermarkets (USD):} Prices rise almost immediately (within 24 hours) when the Riel drops.
    \item \textbf{Local Markets (KHR):} Prices adjust slowly (about 2 weeks) as vendors absorb the change.
    \item \textbf{Utilities (KHR):} Rates are fixed by the government and do not change with the exchange rate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Connecting to Official National History (Splicing)}
Our pipeline starts from 100.0, but the government's official NIS series starts from 2006. To make our numbers useful, we use \textbf{Splicing}:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\text{CPI}}_{\text{NIS, } M} = \text{CPI}_{\text{latest}}^{\text{NIS, 2006}} \times \left( 1.0 + \frac{\pi_{\text{MoM}}}{100.0} \right)
\end{equation}
This ensures that a $\text{CPI} \approx 105.8$ in our system translates to $\text{CPI} \approx 220.5$ on the government scale.

\section{System Reliability: Operational Excellence and Scale}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{The Self-Learning Feedback Loop}
Our system learns from human expertise. When an analyst corrects a product category, the \texttt{automated\_rule\_suggester} finds the pattern and proposes a \textbf{Regex Rule}. Once approved, this rule is added to the database, so the system never makes the same mistake twice.

\subsection{Automated Quality Assurance}
To ensure zero errors, we execute:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{56 dbt Data Tests:} Check that weights sum to 100\%, tariffs match gazettes, and no price is negative.
    \item \textbf{451 Python Tests:} Validate API parsing, TLS spoofing, and formula precision.
\end{itemize}
If any critical test fails, the Airflow pipeline halts immediately, ensuring that corrupted data never reaches the official reports.

\subsection{Database Performance at Scale}
With 13 million records a year, we use \textbf{Declarative Monthly Partitioning}. Data is stored in monthly folders (e.g., \texttt{raw\_prices\_2026\_09}). This allows PostgreSQL to use \textit{partition pruning}, skipping 95\% of historical data and returning query results in under \textbf{8 milliseconds}.

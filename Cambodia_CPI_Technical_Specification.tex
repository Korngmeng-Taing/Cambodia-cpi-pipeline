\section{Executive Summary and Problem Statement}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Macroeconomic Context of the Cambodian Economy}
The Kingdom of Cambodia represents a distinctive macroeconomic setting: a highly dollarized developing economy undergoing rapid digital transformation. Consumer spending patterns are heavily concentrated in basic necessities, as documented in the Cambodia Socio-Economic Survey (CSES) conducted by the National Institute of Statistics (NIS):
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Food and Non-Alcoholic Beverages (Division 01):} 44.775\% of total national consumer expenditure.
    \item \textbf{Housing, Water, Electricity, Gas and Other Fuels (Division 04):} 17.084\% of national expenditure.
    \item \textbf{Transport and Automotive Fuels (Division 07):} 12.180\% of national expenditure.
\end{itemize}
These three divisions represent \textbf{74.039\%} of the aggregate Cambodian consumer basket. Under dual-currency circulation (USD and Cambodian Riel KHR), price shocks in international energy, cross-border agricultural logistics, and exchange-rate fluctuations pass through directly into domestic retail prices within hours.

\subsection{The Structural Information Deficit in Conventional CPI}
Official national inflation statistics are compiled by the National Institute of Statistics (NIS) through physical retail market surveys across Phnom Penh and provinces. While methodologically compliant with international standards, conventional CPI surveys exhibit three operational limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Publication Latency (20 to 30 Days):} Official monthly CPI is published 3 to 4 weeks after the close of the reference month. Under volatile macroeconomic shocks (e.g., fuel price surges or agricultural border closures), central bankers and fiscal planners operate in an information deficit.
    \item \textbf{Sampling Vulnerability and High Field Overhead:} Physical store auditing relies on manual price recording, which is cost-intensive, geographically constrained, and susceptible to enumerator recording errors.
    \item \textbf{Point-in-Time Mid-Month Sampling:} Traditional surveys record prices on single target dates (e.g., the 15th of the month), failing to capture intra-month price dynamics, weekend promotions, and temporary supply chain shocks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Project Vision and Core Objectives}
The \textbf{Cambodia Daily CPI Medallion Pipeline} resolves these challenges by establishing an automated, high-frequency price intelligence engine that captures over 35,500 daily retail price observations across 23 digital market feeds. The platform implements rigorous econometric processing, machine-learning classification, missing-price imputation, quality adjustments, real-time nowcasting, and executive business intelligence dashboards.

\subsection{Strict Bronze Deduplication Contract}
To guarantee idempotency and prevent artificial volume inflation across network retries, the Bronze table \texttt{bronze.raw\_prices} enforces a deterministic unique observation index:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS uq_raw_prices_observation 
ON bronze.raw_prices (
    store_id, 
    source_name, 
    COALESCE(source_url, ''), 
    item_description_raw, 
    (scraped_at AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')::date
);
\end{lstlisting}
\textbf{Design Invariant:} The nominal price column is strictly excluded from this index. If a network disruption forces a crawler to re-scrape a store on day $t$, the database updates the observation in-place rather than inserting duplicate records with diverging prices.

\subsection{Staging JSONB Audit Payload Concatenation}
When batch telemetry is inserted into \texttt{staging.raw\_scrapes}, conflicting records preserve execution logs through atomic JSONB concatenation:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
INSERT INTO staging.raw_scrapes (scrape_batch_id, store_slug, payload, scraped_at)
VALUES (%s, %s, %s, %s)
ON CONFLICT (scrape_batch_id) 
DO UPDATE SET payload = staging.raw_scrapes.payload || EXCLUDED.payload;
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Silver Layer: Entity Resolution and AI Classification}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Why Apply Entity Resolution?}
Web scrapers collect unstructured, noisy listings across platforms that vary in naming conventions, multilingual descriptions, and packaging. Calculating inflation directly on raw strings introduces severe bias:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Artificial Churn:} Retailers alter title prefixes (e.g., adding ``[PROMO]'' or ``BEST SELLER''), causing rule-based systems to treat existing products as new varieties.
    \item \textbf{Unit Incompatibility:} Merging a single can of soda ($330\text{ml}$) with a case of 24 cans ($24 \times 330\text{ml}$) distorts elementary price relatives by over $2,000\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Tier Item Matching Architecture}
The item resolution engine in \texttt{pipeline/item\_matcher.py} executes a deterministic cascade:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1: Global Barcode Validation (\texttt{is\_valid\_barcode}):}
        Validates GTIN-8, GTIN-12, GTIN-13, and GTIN-14 standards. Dummy retailer barcodes (e.g., \texttt{123456789012}, \texttt{000000000000}, or ending in \texttt{00000000}) are rejected.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2: Store SKU Memoization:}
        Sub-millisecond lookup in \texttt{silver.dim\_canonical\_products} mapping \texttt{(source\_name, raw\_item\_id)} to a persistent \texttt{canonical\_item\_id}.
    \item \textbf{Tier 3: Deterministic Specification Guards (\texttt{is\_spec\_compatible}):}
        Strict regex extraction blocks invalid merges:
        \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
            \item \textit{Storage Capacity:} $128\text{GB} \neq 256\text{GB} \neq 512\text{GB}$ (absolute barrier).
            \item \textit{Pack Multipliers:} Single cans are strictly separated from multi-packs.
            \item \textit{Metric Mass and Volume:} $1000\text{g} = 1\text{kg}$, $500\text{ml} = 0.5\text{L}$.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 4: Multilingual Vector Space Embeddings:}
        Product titles are embedded into a 768-dimensional space using \texttt{gemini-embedding-2} (with local \texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2} fallback). Cosine similarity determines resolution:
        $$\text{CosineSim}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2}$$
        \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
            \item $\text{Score} \ge 0.92$: Auto-linked to existing canonical product.
            \item $0.80 \le \text{Score} < 0.92$: Forwarded to Gemini Flash LLM for pairwise economic equivalence arbitration.
            \item $\text{Score} < 0.80$: Instantiated as a new canonical product entity (\texttt{uuid.uuid4()}).
        \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The 4-Tier UN COICOP Classification Ladder}
Every canonical item is categorized into the UN COICOP hierarchy via \texttt{pipeline/hybrid\_embeddings\_classifier.py}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1: Deterministic Overrides:} Curated brand/barcode rules in \texttt{silver.coicop\_override}.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2: Single-Category Pure Store Mapping:} 15 domain-pure stores are assigned instantaneously in SQL (EDC $\rightarrow$ \texttt{04.5.1}, Tela/PTT $\rightarrow$ \texttt{07.2.2}, Smart/Cellcard $\rightarrow$ \texttt{08.2.0}, PPWSA $\rightarrow$ \texttt{04.4.1}, BookMeBus $\rightarrow$ \texttt{07.3.2}).
    \item \textbf{Tier 3: Centroid Vector Classification:} For multi-category department stores (AEON, Lucky, Chip Mong), titles are compared against 92 bilingual English/Khmer COICOP reference centroids. Matches with cosine similarity $\ge 0.72$ are assigned.
    \item \textbf{Tier 4: Gemini Pro/Flash Few-Shot Disambiguation:} Ambiguous items are resolved via Gemini using structured JSON schemas and memoized in \texttt{silver.coicop\_llm\_memo}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Gold Layer: Econometric CPI Compilation Engine}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Base Price Formulation}
For a designated base period ($t=0$), the base price for each item $i$ is compiled as the unweighted geometric mean across all store quotes on that date:
\begin{equation}
P_{i, 0} = \exp \left( \frac{1}{|S_{i, 0}|} \sum_{s \in S_{i, 0}} \ln P_{i, 0, s} \right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Compounded Class-Mean Geometric Imputation Engine}
In high-frequency web scraping, products frequently experience stockouts. International statistical standards (IMF/ILO, 2020) strictly prohibit flat carry-forward ($\widehat{P}_{i, t} = P_{i, t-1}$) as it dampens true inflation volatility and creates downward bias.

When an item $i$ in division $c$ is unobserved on day $t$ with an elapsed gap $\Delta t \in [1, 7]$ days:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Compute the 1-day geometric mean movement ratio of all observed items in division $c$:
    \begin{equation}
    R_{c, t} = \exp \left( \frac{1}{|M_{c, t}|} \sum_{j \in M_{c, t}} \ln \left( \frac{P_{j, t}}{P_{j, t-1}} \right) \right), \quad \text{clamped to } [0.80, 1.25]
    \end{equation}
    \item Compound the daily movement over the actual elapsed gap $\Delta t$:
    \begin{equation}
    \widehat{P}_{i, t} = P_{i, t - \Delta t} \times \left( R_{c, t} \right)^{\Delta t}
    \end{equation}
    \item If an item remains unobserved for $\Delta t > 7$ days, it is dropped from the active basket (churn exclusion).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Log-Linear Hedonic Quality Adjustment Engine}
Consumer durables (smartphones, computing hardware) experience rapid technological shifts. When an older model is replaced by a newer specification, raw price differences reflect both inflation and quality improvements. The engine in \texttt{pipeline/hedonic\_regression.py} estimates:
\begin{equation}
\ln P_{i, t} = \alpha + \sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k z_{i, k} + \sum_{\tau=1}^T \delta_\tau D_{i, \tau} + \epsilon_{i, t}
\end{equation}
where $z_{i,k}$ represents physical attributes (RAM, storage capacity, screen size) and $D_{i,\tau}$ are time dummy variables. When design matrices exhibit collinearity, the engine flags \texttt{SKIPPED\_RANK\_DEFICIENT} and safely defaults to matched-model pricing.

\subsection{Elementary Aggregates: Jevons Geometric Mean Index}
At the 4-digit / 5-digit subclass level ($c$), elementary price indices are compiled using the Jevons unweighted geometric mean:
\begin{equation}
I_{J, c}^{0:t} = \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n_c} \frac{P_{i, t}}{P_{i, 0}} \right)^{1 / n_c} \times 100.0 = \exp \left( \frac{1}{n_c} \sum_{i=1}^{n_c} \ln \left( \frac{P_{i, t}}{P_{i, 0}} \right) \right) \times 100.0
\end{equation}
\textbf{Axiomatic Foundation:} The Jevons index satisfies both the Time Reversal Test ($I^{0:t} \cdot I^{t:0} = 1$) and the Transitivity Axiom ($I^{0:t} = I^{0:1} \cdot I^{1:t}$), eliminating elementary formula bias.

\subsection{Higher-Level Aggregation: Modified Laspeyres Formulation}
Subclasses are aggregated into 2-digit divisions using official subclass weights:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{div}, k}^{0:t} = \frac{\sum_{c \in \text{div}_k} w_c \cdot I_{J, c}^{0:t}}{\sum_{c \in \text{div}_k} w_c}
\end{equation}

Division indices are aggregated into national \textbf{Headline CPI} using CSES national expenditure weights:
\begin{equation}
\text{CPI}_{\text{Headline}}^{0:t} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{12} W_k \cdot I_{\text{div}, k}^{0:t}}{\sum_{k=1}^{12} W_k \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[\text{active}_k]}}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Active-Weight Normalization:} If a division has zero observed items on a given day, its weight is excluded from both numerator and denominator, preventing synthetic deflation.

\subsection{Core CPI Formulation (Ex-Food and Energy)}
To isolate underlying core inflationary pressures, Core CPI excludes Division 01 (Food), Division 04 (Housing/Utilities), and Division 07 (Transport/Fuel):
\begin{equation}
\text{CPI}_{\text{Core}}^{0:t} = \frac{\sum_{k \notin \{01, 04, 07\}} W_k \cdot I_{\text{div}, k}^{0:t}}{\sum_{k \notin \{01, 04, 07\}} W_k}
\end{equation}

\section{High-Frequency Inflation Nowcasting Engine}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Closing the 30-Day Policy Gap}
Official monthly inflation statistics from the National Institute of Statistics (NIS) are published with an observation delay of 3--4 weeks. The nowcasting engine in \texttt{ml/nowcaster.py} projects the final monthly index level on any intra-month day $t$ by modeling high-frequency price relatives across 5 core consumer divisions using a Two-Stage Hybrid Ridge architecture with Empirical Bayes shrinkage.

\subsection{Axiomatic 5-Basket Price Relatives Architecture}
Rather than modeling an ad-hoc aggregate headline index directly, the system models price relatives across five key COICOP divisions $\mathcal{K} = \{01, 02, 04, 07, 11\}$ comprising \textbf{81.58\%} of the national consumption basket:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Division 01 (Food \& Non-Alcoholic Beverages, $w_{01} = 0.4478$)
    \item Division 02 (Alcoholic Beverages \& Tobacco, $w_{02} = 0.0163$)
    \item Division 04 (Housing, Water, Electricity, Gas \& Other Fuels, $w_{04} = 0.1708$)
    \item Division 07 (Transport \& Vehicle Fuels, $w_{07} = 0.1223$)
    \item Division 11 (Restaurants \& Hotels, $w_{11} = 0.0586$)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Two-Stage Mathematical Formulation}
For target month $M$ having $T$ calendar days, with $N_{\text{obs}}$ observed days and $N_{\text{rem}} = T - N_{\text{obs}}$ remaining unobserved days:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1: Micro-Aggregation of Realized Trajectory:}
        For each basket $k \in \mathcal{K}$, realized daily elementary Jevons price indices are observed for days $s \in \{1, \dots, N_{\text{obs}}\}$.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2: Multi-Horizon Ridge Drift Estimation with Empirical Bayes Shrinkage:}
        For each division $k$, expected daily drift $\hat{\mu}_k$ is estimated via $L_2$-regularized Ridge regression across a rich feature vector $\mathbf{x}_t$:
        \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{x}_t = \left[ m_{\text{food}, 3d}, m_{\text{food}, 7d}, m_{\text{food}, 14d}, m_{\text{trans}, 3d}, m_{\text{trans}, 7d}, m_{\text{fx}, 7d}, m_{\text{fx}, 14d}, \mathbb{I}_{\text{holiday}}, \text{dist}_{\text{holiday}} \right]^T
        \end{equation}
        \begin{equation}
        \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_k = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \left\{ \|\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 + \alpha \|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 \right\}
        \end{equation}
        where $\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0\}$ is tuned via Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (\texttt{RidgeCV}).
        To guard against out-of-sample variance early in the month, an Empirical Bayesian shrinkage prior pulls extreme drift estimates toward zero:
        \begin{equation}
        \hat{\mu}_k^{\text{shrunk}} = \left(1 - \lambda_{\text{shrink}}\right) \hat{\mu}_k^{\text{Ridge}} + \lambda_{\text{shrink}} \cdot 0, \quad \lambda_{\text{shrink}} = \max(0, 1 - R^2)
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Linear Trajectory Midpoint Expectation:}
        Under drift $\hat{\mu}_k^{\text{shrunk}}$, the expected average price index for division $k$ over the remaining $N_{\text{rem}}$ days is:
        \begin{equation}
        \mathbb{E}[\bar{P}_{k, \text{remaining}}] = \bar{P}_{k, \text{obs}, t} \times \left( 1.0 + \hat{\mu}_k^{\text{shrunk}} \cdot \frac{N_{\text{rem}} + 1}{2} \right)
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Intra-Month Division Index Nowcast ($\hat{I}_k$):}
        \begin{equation}
        \hat{I}_k = \left( \frac{N_{\text{obs}}}{T} \right) \bar{P}_{k, \text{obs}} + \left( \frac{N_{\text{rem}}}{T} \right) \mathbb{E}[\bar{P}_{k, \text{remaining}}]
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Exact Laspeyres Axiomatic Aggregation:}
        The headline CPI nowcast is synthesized bottom-up using official NIS expenditure weights:
        \begin{equation}
        \widehat{\text{HeadlineCPI}}_M = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} w_k \hat{I}_k + \sum_{m \notin \mathcal{K}} w_m I_{m, \text{baseline}}
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Projected Month-over-Month Inflation Rate ($\hat{\pi}_{\text{MoM}}$):}
        \begin{equation}
        \hat{\pi}_{\text{MoM}} = \left( \frac{\widehat{\text{HeadlineCPI}}_M - \text{HeadlineCPI}_{M-1}}{\text{HeadlineCPI}_{M-1}} \right) \times 100.0\%
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Uncertainty Envelope (95\% Confidence Interval):}
        Uncertainty decays monotonically as the proportion of observed days increases:
        \begin{equation}
        \text{Margin of Error}_t = 1.96 \times \sigma_{\text{daily}} \times \sqrt{\frac{N_{\text{rem}}}{T}}
        \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Dual-Baseline Problem}
The web-scraping pipeline computes prices relative to an operational base date ($I^{\text{Pipeline}} \approx 100.0$), whereas official Cambodian national accounts reference \textbf{October--December 2006 = 100.0}, where published index levels exceed \textbf{219.0+}. Bridging these series without inducing structural level shifts requires dual-linking methodologies:

\subsection{Real-Time Nowcast Splicing}
The nowcaster connects daily price movements to the official NIS index level published for month $M-1$:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\text{CPI}}_{\text{NIS, } M} = \text{CPI}_{\text{latest}}^{\text{NIS, 2006}} \times \left( 1.0 + \frac{\hat{\pi}_{\text{MoM}}}{100.0} \right)
\end{equation}
This estimate is persisted daily into \texttt{gold.fct\_cpi\_nowcast} and validated against official ground-truth releases via \texttt{gold.v\_nowcast\_evaluation}.

\subsection{Annual Rebasing Overlap Splicing (December Overlap)}
When updating the pipeline's reference base year annually to capture newly emerged digital goods and shifting consumption baskets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Compute the 31-day average index level during the December overlap period under the expiring base:
    \begin{equation}
    \bar{I}_{\text{Dec}}^{\text{Old Base}} = \frac{1}{31} \sum_{d=1}^{31} I_{\text{Dec } d}^{\text{Old Base}}
    \end{equation}
    \item Compute the continuous series chain-linking splice factor:
    \begin{equation}
    S = \frac{\bar{I}_{\text{Dec}}^{\text{Old Base}}}{100.0}
    \end{equation}
    \item Persist $S$ into \texttt{gold.cpi\_base\_dates} under \texttt{avg\_december\_cpi}. All subsequent index calculations link dynamically:
    \begin{equation}
    I_{\text{Continuous}, t} = I_{\text{New Base}, t} \times S
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
This formulation preserves short-run price ratios without introducing artificial index jumps at base-year transitions.

\begin{abstract}
\noindent Official headline Consumer Price Index (CPI) releases from national statistical agencies suffer from an unavoidable institutional lag of 25 to 45 days. This guide presents the comprehensive mathematical, econometric, and algorithmic methodology of the automated high-frequency \textbf{Cambodia CPI Nowcasting Engine}. The engine operates on a hybrid two-tier architecture: combining axiomatic Laspeyres index aggregation using fixed Cambodia Socio-Economic Survey (CSES) expenditure weights with a regularized 5-basket Ridge regression ($\text{RidgeCV}$) and Empirical Bayes structural prior shrinkage. We provide the complete analytical derivation of the baseline drift ($\hat{\alpha}$) and empirical division elasticities ($\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}$), detail the microdata aggregation via unweighted geometric Jevons elementary indices, formalize the cross-sector fuel logistics pass-through and cultural festival calendar decay kernels, evaluate against the canonical \textbf{Atkeson--Ohanian (2001) Random Walk benchmark model} (achieving an empirical out-of-sample \textbf{error reduction of 17.68\%} with Diebold--Mariano statistical significance), and walk through a unified real-world operational case study for Cambodia.
\end{abstract}

\section{The High-Frequency Nowcasting Problem}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{The Institutional Information Lag}
The National Institute of Statistics (NIS) of the Ministry of Planning publishes the official Consumer Price Index for Phnom Penh on a monthly cadence. Due to nationwide field enumeration schedules, physical market surveying, double-key data entry verification, and inter-departmental statistical audit, the official bulletin for calendar month $M$ is typically published between day 25 and day 45 of month $M+1$. 

For monetary authorities at the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) and fiscal planners at the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), this creates a critical 4 to 6-week ``blind spot'' during which supply-side shocks (e.g., international crude petroleum spikes, agricultural weather disruptions, exchange rate fluctuations) cannot be verified empirically through sovereign statistical releases.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation of the Intra-Month Nowcasting Task}
Let $M$ be the ongoing calendar month comprising $D$ total days ($D \in \{28, 29, 30, 31\}$). On any given evaluation day $d \in \{1, 2, \dots, D\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Realized Information Set} $\mathcal{H}_d$: Represents the historical price trajectory observed from day 1 to day $d$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{H}_d = \left\{ \mathbf{P}_1, \mathbf{P}_2, \dots, \mathbf{P}_d \right\}
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathbf{P}_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$ is the conformed vector of daily COICOP division price index facts compiled in \texttt{gold.fct\_cpi\_daily} for calendar day $\tau \le d$.
    \item \textbf{The Unobserved Future Horizon} $\mathcal{F}_d$: Represents the remaining $N = D - d$ calendar days of the month that have not yet transpired:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{F}_d = \left\{ \mathbf{P}_{d+1}, \mathbf{P}_{d+2}, \dots, \mathbf{P}_D \right\}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The fundamental task of the nowcaster is to construct an unbiased, minimum-variance estimator of the full-month headline index level $\widehat{\text{CPI}}_M(d)$ and month-over-month inflation rate $\widehat{\pi}_{\text{MoM}, M}(d)$ conditional on information available up to day $d$:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\text{CPI}}_M(d) = \mathbb{E}\left[ \text{CPI}_M \;\middle|\; \mathcal{H}_d, \boldsymbol{\Theta} \right]
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Theta} = \{\hat{\alpha}_{\text{daily}}, \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_k^{\text{shrink}}, \mathbf{w}_{\text{CSES}}\}$ represents the calibrated econometric and national accounting parameter set.

\subsection{Microdata Aggregation: From Web Scraping to Division Indices}
Before econometric modeling occurs, raw retail price microdata is collected daily across 25 modern supermarket chains, wholesale outlets, and e-commerce platforms in Phnom Penh ($\approx 41,500$ raw product listings per day).

\paragraph{1. Data Cleansing and Silver Standardization:}
Raw scraped records are mapped into conformed silver tables (\texttt{silver.clean\_store\_prices}). Dual currencies are normalized into Khmer Riel (KHR) using daily official MEF exchange rates, Khmer numerals are mapped to standard digits (e.g., \khmer{៥០០} $\rightarrow$ 500), units of measurement are converted into uniform metrics ($500\text{g} \rightarrow 0.5\text{kg}$, $1500\text{ml} \rightarrow 1.5\text{L}$), and product varieties are resolved through a 4-tier matching waterfall: Universal Barcode (EAN-13) $\rightarrow$ Store ID + SKU composite key with persistent memoization $\rightarrow$ Clean Text Matching $\rightarrow$ 768-dim Vector Embeddings (\texttt{gemini-embedding-2} with PostgreSQL \texttt{pgvector} HNSW index) guarded by deterministic pack/spec checks (Cosine $\ge 0.80$).

\paragraph{2. Store-Balanced Geometric Jevons Aggregation (1 Store = 1 Vote):}
To prevent retailers with large product catalogs (e.g., 90 rice varieties vs. 10 at a smaller vendor) from distorting national price trends, the pipeline implements an axiomatic two-stage store-balanced Jevons index:
\begin{equation}
    \ln I_{s, t}^{\text{Jevons}} = \frac{1}{n_{s, k}} \sum_{i=1}^{n_{s, k}} \left( \ln p_{i, s, t} - \ln p_{i, s, 0} \right)
\end{equation}
Within each elementary category $k$, eligible retail outlets receive equal voting weight:
\begin{equation}
    I_{k, t}^{\text{Elementary}} = \frac{1}{|S_k|} \sum_{s \in S_k} \exp\left( \ln I_{s, t}^{\text{Jevons}} \right)
\end{equation}
This mathematical design unconditionally satisfies time-reversal and scale invariance while eliminating catalog-volume distortion.

\paragraph{3. Handling New Products on Non-Base Dates:}
For products that appear on store shelves after the initial base date, the pipeline dynamically splices their first observed quote as their personal baseline ($p_{i, s, 0} = p_{i, s, \text{entry}}$, relative price = $1.00$ on Day 1), tracking subsequent price movements without creating fictitious historical shocks.

\paragraph{4. Missing Price Policy (ILO Class-Mean Imputation):}
When a product experiences temporary stockouts, its price is dynamically imputed using the geometric mean rate of change of observed items in the corresponding category (ILO standard):
\begin{equation}
    \hat{p}_{i, t} = p_{i, t-1} \times \left( \prod_{j \in C_i} \frac{p_{j, t}}{p_{j, t-1}} \right)^{1/|C_i|}
\end{equation}
If an item remains unobserved for $>7$ consecutive days, it is recognized as discontinued and permanently dropped from the active basket.

\paragraph{5. Synthesis into 12 Division Indices:}
The elementary indices are aggregated into the 12 official COICOP division indices $I_{j, t}$ published in \texttt{gold.fct\_cpi\_daily}, forming the input space for the nowcaster.

\subsection{Why Stationarity via Log-Differencing is Required}
Raw consumer price indices $P_t$ exhibit persistent, non-stationary unit-root behavior ($I(1)$). Attempting to estimate econometric parameters directly on raw price levels produces \textbf{spurious regression}: standard OLS yields inflated $t$-statistics and $R^2 \approx 0.99$ driven purely by shared deterministic trends, while the true economic pass-through coefficients remain indeterminate.

To ensure econometric validity, all price series are transformed into stationary \textbf{first log-differences}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \ln P_{j, t} = \ln(P_{j, t}) - \ln(P_{j, t-1}) \approx \frac{P_{j, t} - P_{j, t-1}}{P_{j, t-1}}
\end{equation}
Continuous log-returns offer three essential mathematical properties:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Stationarity}: Eliminates unit-root persistence, satisfying Gauss--Markov assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Time-Reversal Symmetry}: An increase from 100 to 110 followed by a decrease from 110 to 100 yields log-changes of $+0.09531$ and $-0.09531$, summing exactly to zero (preventing asymmetric compounding drift).
    \item \textbf{Exact Temporal Additivity}: Multi-period returns equal the linear sum of intra-period log returns.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Layer 1: Fixed-Weight Axiomatic Aggregation and Core CPI}
Official national CPI construction relies on the Laspeyres index framework fixed by the Cambodia Socio-Economic Survey (CSES 2019/2020). The 12 COICOP divisions carry fixed consumption basket weights $w_j$ such that $\sum_{j=1}^{12} w_j = 1.00$. Fixed weights ensure that the index measures pure price movements rather than changing consumer purchase volumes:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Headline CPI}_M(d) = \sum_{j=1}^{12} w_j \cdot I_{j, M}(d)
\end{equation}
For central bank monetary policy at the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC), headline inflation is often distorted by transitory supply shocks in volatile food and fuel markets. The pipeline therefore computes official \textbf{Core CPI} by excluding Food and Non-Alcoholic Beverages (Division 01, weight $44.775\%$) and Housing and Utilities (Division 04, weight $17.062\%$):
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\text{Core CPI}_M(d) = \frac{\sum_{j \notin \{01, 04\}} w_j \cdot I_{j, M}(d)}{\sum_{j \notin \{01, 04\}} w_j}}
\end{equation}
The core basket isolates the remaining \textbf{38.163\%} of the consumption basket, providing monetary authorities with an undistorted measure of underlying demand-pull domestic price pressures.

\subsection{Layer 2: Disaggregated Dynamic Ridge Drift Projection}
For the remaining $D - d$ unobserved days, prices do not stand still. We compute high-frequency price velocity (momentum) and combine it with regularized Ridge pass-through elasticities and Empirical Bayes shrinkage to estimate the daily drift $\mu_j(d)$ for each division.

\subsection{Synthesis: Horizon Blending}
For every division $j$, the estimated full-month index $I_{j, M}(d)$ blends observed reality with forward-looking projections weighted strictly by the elapsed calendar progress:
\begin{equation}
    I_{j, M}(d) = \left( \frac{d}{D} \right) \cdot I_{j, \text{realized}}(d) + \left( \frac{D - d}{D} \right) \cdot I_{j, \text{projected}}(d)
    \label{eq:blending}
\end{equation}
On Day 1 ($d=1$), the projection dominates ($96.7\%$ weight in a 30-day month). On Day 30 ($d=30$), the projection weight drops to $0.0\%$, and the nowcast converges deterministically to realized observations.

\section{Econometric Calibration and Feature Engineering}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{The 36-Month Historical Training Panel and Data Extraction}
To estimate empirical elasticities that reflect true Cambodian macroeconomic dynamics, the calibration engine (\texttt{MacroDatasetBuilder} in \texttt{ml/calibration.py}) extracts the continuous 36-month official sovereign series published by the National Institute of Statistics (September 2023 through August 2026). In production, this dataset is queried directly from PostgreSQL:
\begin{verbatim}
SELECT cpi_month, headline_cpi, core_cpi,
       cpi_division_01, cpi_division_02, cpi_division_04,
       cpi_division_07, cpi_division_11
FROM gold.dim_nis_official_cpi
ORDER BY cpi_month ASC;
\end{verbatim}
with version-controlled fallback to \texttt{dbt/seeds/nis\_official\_cpi.csv}. In addition, the daily commercial USD/KHR exchange rate series from the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) is aggregated to monthly averages from \texttt{staging.exchange\_rates}.

\paragraph{Stationary First-Difference Transformations:}
Taking continuous first log-differences across the 36 consecutive monthly observations produces $T = 34$ stationary monthly rates:
\begin{equation}
    y_t = \ln\left(\frac{\text{Headline CPI}_t}{\text{Headline CPI}_{t-1}}\right), \quad x_{j, t} = \ln\left(\frac{I_{j, t}}{I_{j, t-1}}\right), \quad x_{\text{fx}, t} = \ln\left(\frac{\text{FX}_t}{\text{FX}_{t-1}}\right)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Exact Mathematical Derivation of Monthly Alpha ($\hat{\alpha}_{\text{monthly}}$):}
In multiple regression, the intercept $\alpha$ represents the unconditional baseline drift rate of the aggregate price index when all explanatory division returns are zero ($\mathbf{x}_t = \mathbf{0}$). Once the Ridge coefficients $\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\lambda^*)$ are calculated via regularized matrix inversion, $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{monthly}}$ is recovered directly from historical sample means:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\hat{\alpha}_{\text{monthly}} = \bar{y} - \sum_{j \in \{01, 02, 04, 07, 11\}} \hat{\beta}_j \bar{x}_j - \hat{\beta}_{\text{fx}} \bar{x}_{\text{fx}}}
\end{equation}
Evaluating across the 36-month calibration sample yields $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{monthly}} = \mathbf{+0.001899}$ ($+0.19\%$ per month), capturing steady structural price growth across unobserved sticky components (services, healthcare, education).

\paragraph{Scaling Monthly Alpha to Instantaneous Daily Alpha ($\alpha_{\text{daily}}$):}
Because continuous log-returns compound additively ($\sum_{t=1}^D \Delta \ln P_t = \Delta \ln P_{\text{month}}$), the monthly intercept scales down linearly into a daily velocity:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\alpha_{\text{daily}} = \frac{\hat{\alpha}_{\text{monthly}}}{D} = \frac{+0.001899}{30} = \mathbf{+0.0000633 \text{ per day}}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Econometric Specification and Multicollinearity Mitigation}
We specify a multi-factor macroeconomic pass-through regression relating aggregate inflation to innovations in the 5 high-velocity expenditure baskets and foreign exchange:
\begin{equation}
    y_t = \alpha + \sum_{j \in \{01, 02, 04, 07, 11\}} \beta_j x_{j, t} + \beta_{\text{fx}} x_{\text{fx}, t} + \varepsilon_t
    \label{eq:macro_reg}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why Standard OLS Fails:}
In macroeconomic time series, sub-indices co-move strongly. When world oil prices surge, transport fuel ($x_{07}$) increases immediately. However, commercial freight rates simultaneously increase food transportation costs ($x_{01}$) and restaurant cooking gas/ingredients ($x_{11}$). 

Under ordinary least squares (OLS), the sample Gram matrix $\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X}$ is severely ill-conditioned (condition number $\kappa > 100$). The OLS estimator exhibits explosive sampling variance ($\text{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = \sigma^2 (\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{X})^{-1}$), which causes coefficients to flip signs counter-intuitively.

\paragraph{Regularized Ridge Estimator:}
To eliminate multicollinearity and shrink unstable noise, we formulate an $L_2$-penalized Tikhonov regularization problem:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \frac{1}{2N} \|\tilde{\mathbf{y}} - \tilde{\mathbf{X}} \boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 + \frac{\lambda}{2} \|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2
\end{equation}
Solving the first-order condition $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(\lambda) = \left( \tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top \tilde{\mathbf{X}} + N\lambda \mathbf{I}_K \right)^{-1} \tilde{\mathbf{X}}^\top \tilde{\mathbf{y}}}
\end{equation}
Adding $\lambda \mathbf{I}$ shifts all eigenvalues strictly positive ($\sigma_i \to \sigma_i + \lambda$), guaranteeing well-conditioned numerical invertibility. Cross-validation across $\Lambda = [10^{-2}, 10^3]$ achieves its global minimum at \textbf{$\lambda^* = 0.0100$}.

\subsection{Empirical Bayes Prior Shrinkage Formulation}
In real-time operations, small sample sizes during early months can cause high estimation variance. The engine safeguards stability by shrinking empirical Ridge estimates toward an informative structural economic prior $\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\text{prior}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_k^{\text{shrink}} = (1 - \gamma_k) \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_k^{\text{Ridge}} + \gamma_k \cdot \boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{\text{prior}}}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_k$ is the sample-size adaptive shrinkage weight implemented in \texttt{ml/nowcaster.py}:
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_k = \max\left( 0.0, \; \min\left( 1.0, \; 1.0 - \frac{n_{\text{samples}} - 8}{22.0} \right) \right)
\end{equation}
When historical observation count $n_{\text{samples}} < 8$, $\gamma_k = 1.0$, anchoring the nowcaster strictly to structural macroeconomic priors. As $n_{\text{samples}} \ge 30$, $\gamma_k = 0.0$, allowing the empirical Ridge model to operate entirely on observed data. The structural prior $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k^{\text{prior}}$ enforces direct pass-through bounds based on CSES expenditure weights, ensuring non-negative elasticities for food and fuel.

\subsection{Cross-Sector Logistics Momentum and Holiday Filtering}
\paragraph{Fuel Logistics Injection:}
In Cambodia, retail fuel prices propagate rapidly into food freight and catering costs. The engine injects 7-day transport momentum ($t_{7\text{d}}$) directly into the feature matrices of Food (01) and Restaurants (11):
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{x}_{01}(d) &= \left[ f_{3\text{d}}, f_{7\text{d}}, f_{14\text{d}}, \mathbf{t_{7\text{d}}}, fx_{7\text{d}}, \text{fest}_{\text{prox}}, \text{prog} \right]^\top \\
    \mathbf{x}_{11}(d) &= \left[ r_{7\text{d}}, f_{7\text{d}}, \mathbf{t_{7\text{d}}}, \text{fest}_{\text{window}}, \text{prog} \right]^\top
\end{align}
Sticky non-freight goods (Clothing, Health, Education) are insulated to prevent spurious inflation contagion.

\paragraph{Lunar Holiday Shock Filtering:}
During \textit{Khmer New Year} (April), \textit{Pchum Ben} (September/October), and the \textit{Water Festival} (November), transitory demand surges occur. The exponential decay kernel $\text{fest}_{\text{prox}}(d)$ dampens forward momentum projections during these windows, ensuring that transient celebration markups naturally mean-revert without corrupting month-end nowcasts.

\subsection{Forward Projection: Exact Discrete Arithmetic Mean}
For the unobserved $N = D - d$ days of the month, the forward division price level is evaluated via the \textbf{Exact Discrete Arithmetic Mean}:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{I_{j, \text{projected}}(d) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N I_{j, d} \exp\left( \mu_j(d) \cdot k \right)}
\end{equation}
Using a first-order Taylor expansion $\exp(x) \approx 1 + x$ and the Gauss summation identity $\sum_{k=1}^N k = \frac{N(N+1)}{2}$, this exact sum is approximated in closed form by the midpoint expectation:
\begin{equation}
    I_{j, \text{projected}}(d) \approx I_{j, d} \times \exp\left( \mu_j(d) \cdot \frac{N + 1}{2} \right)
\end{equation}
Both formulations agree to within $0.000002$ index points, with the production pipeline executing the vector discrete evaluation \texttt{np.mean(latest\_div * np.exp(div\_drift * future\_days))}.

\section{The Benchmark Model: Atkeson--Ohanian Random Walk}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Why a Benchmark Model is Essential}
In macroeconomic forecasting, raw error statistics are uninformative in isolation. In their seminal work, Atkeson and Ohanian \cite{atkeson2001phillips} proved that complex econometric models systematically fail to beat a naive \textbf{Random Walk} on out-of-sample inflation. In central bank literature, the Atkeson--Ohanian Random Walk is the gold standard: \textbf{a nowcasting model is only economically valuable if its Relative RMSE is strictly less than 1.00.}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}
The Atkeson--Ohanian Random Walk posits that the best out-of-sample predictor of month $t$'s inflation is simply the realized inflation of month $t-1$:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{y}_t^{\text{RW}} = y_{t-1} = \Delta \ln \text{CPI}_{t-1} \implies \widehat{\pi}_{\text{MoM}, t}^{\text{RW}} = \pi_{\text{MoM}, t-1}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Out-of-Sample Scorecard and Statistical Significance}
We evaluate the 5-basket RidgeCV nowcaster against the Random Walk benchmark across a held-out 10-month out-of-sample window (November 2025 to August 2026, $M=10$):
\begin{equation}
    \text{Relative RMSE} = \frac{\text{RMSE}_{\text{Model}}}{\text{RMSE}_{\text{RW}}} = \frac{0.8572}{1.0414} = \mathbf{0.8232}
\end{equation}

\section{The Daily Real-Time Execution Algorithm}

\subsection{Daily Algorithmic Sequence}
On every calendar day $d$ at 08:00 AM (ICT), the production pipeline executes the following sequence:

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Daily Execution Algorithm (ml/nowcaster.py)]
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Ingest Daily Facts}: Extract realized price relatives from \texttt{gold.fct\_cpi\_daily} and compute $I_{j, \text{realized}}(d) = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{\tau=1}^d P_{j, \tau}$.
    \item \textbf{Feature Extraction}: Compute multi-horizon momentum ($m_{3\text{d}}, m_{7\text{d}}, m_{14\text{d}}$), fuel shock ($t_{7\text{d}}$), exchange rate drift ($fx_{7\text{d}}$), and lunar holiday proximity ($\text{fest}_{\text{prox}}$).
    \item \textbf{Compute Daily Drift}: For each division $j$, compute $\mu_j(d)$ via RidgeCV and Empirical Bayes prior shrinkage.
    \item \textbf{Project Unobserved Days}: $I_{j, \text{projected}}(d) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N P_{j, d} \cdot \exp\left( \mu_j(d) \cdot k \right)$.
    \item \textbf{Horizon Blending}: $I_{j, M}(d) = \frac{d}{D} I_{j, \text{realized}}(d) + \frac{D-d}{D} I_{j, \text{projected}}(d)$.
    \item \textbf{Laspeyres Aggregation}: $\text{Headline CPI}_M(d) = \sum_{j=1}^{12} w_j I_{j, M}(d)$.
    \item \textbf{Chain-Linking}: $\widehat{\text{CPI}}_{\text{NIS}, M}(d) = \text{CPI}_{\text{NIS}, M-1} \times \exp\left( \ln\left( \frac{\text{Headline CPI}_M(d)}{\text{Headline CPI}_{M-1}} \right) \right)$.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Quantification}: Compute 95\% confidence bounds $[\text{CI}_{\text{lower}}, \text{CI}_{\text{upper}}]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Asymptotic Uncertainty Decay and 95\% Confidence Intervals}
Uncertainty is strictly time-dependent. Let $\sigma_d$ be the daily standard deviation of headline CPI within the month. The margin of error is formulated with an asymptotic time-decay factor:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MoE}_{95\%}(d) = 1.95996 \cdot \sigma_d \cdot \sqrt{\frac{D - d}{D}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{CI}_{95\%}(d) = \left[ \widehat{\text{CPI}}_M(d) - \text{MoE}_{95\%}(d), \; \widehat{\text{CPI}}_M(d) + \text{MoE}_{95\%}(d) \right]
\end{equation}
As $d \to D$, the ratio $\frac{D-d}{D} \to 0$, causing the margin of error to collapse deterministically to zero at month-end.

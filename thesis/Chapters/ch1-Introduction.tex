\section{Macroeconomic Background and Motivation}
\hs The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is the fundamental macroeconomic metric used worldwide to quantify changes in the cost of living, track inflationary pressures, guide central bank monetary policy, adjust public wages, calibrate social safety nets, and index national financial contracts. In Cambodia, the official Consumer Price Index is compiled and published by the National Institute of Statistics (NIS) within the Ministry of Planning, adhering to the United Nations Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP) hierarchy.

\subsection{Structural Dynamics of the Cambodian Economy}
\hs Monitoring consumer price movements in Cambodia is uniquely complex due to specific structural characteristics of the domestic economy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extensive Dollarization and Dual-Currency Dynamics:} The Cambodian retail economy operates under high dollarization, with both Cambodian Riel (KHR) and US Dollars (USD) circulating simultaneously. E-commerce platforms, supermarkets, and service providers price goods in both currencies. Consequently, daily exchange rate fluctuations directly and immediately pass through to the retail cost of imported consumer goods.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Expenditure Concentration in Food and Energy:} According to the official NIS expenditure surveys, \textbf{Division 01 (Food and Non-Alcoholic Beverages) accounts for 44.800\%} of the total national consumer basket. Within food, basic staples dominate household expenditures: \textbf{Rice and Cereals alone represent 18.500\%}, Meat accounts for \textbf{10.200\%}, and Fresh Fish represents \textbf{5.800\%}. Furthermore, \textbf{Division 04 (Housing, Utilities, Gas) contributes 17.100\%} and \textbf{Division 07 (Transport and Fuel) contributes 12.200\%}. Because over $74\%$ of the national basket is concentrated in food, utilities, and transport, global supply chain shocks or regional fuel spikes create acute price surges that disproportionately impact vulnerable households.
    \item \textbf{Import Dependency for Essential Consumer Goods:} Cambodia relies heavily on imported refined petroleum fuels, packaged pharmaceuticals, telecommunications equipment, and manufactured household goods. Changes in international logistics costs or foreign exchange rates impact domestic shelves with high velocity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of Traditional Monthly Manual Survey Methodologies}
\hs The conventional methodology utilized by national statistical agencies worldwide—and historically in Cambodia—relies upon physical price enumerators conducting monthly in-person field surveys at designated physical wet markets and retail stores. While methodologically established, this approach entails critical structural limitations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Significant Temporal Publication Lag (30 to 60 Days):} Monthly survey data requires multi-week collection, manual data verification, clerical entry, and aggregation. As a result, official monthly CPI figures are published 3 to 6 weeks after the reference month ends, creating a retrospective "rearview mirror" view of the economy.
    \item \textbf{Intra-Month Volatility Blind Spots:} Monthly sampling typically observes prices on a single designated day (or mid-month interval). It entirely misses intra-month volatility, promotional discount cycles, weekend price spikes, and rapid price adjustments following mid-month exchange rate devaluations or fuel price resets.
    \item \textbf{Substantial Operational and Budgetary Overhead:} Deploying field enumerators across multiple provinces requires significant logistical planning, transport expenses, supervisory overhead, and paper-based data audits, making frequent (daily or weekly) national updates economically impossible through traditional means.
    \item \textbf{Sampling Granularity Constraints:} Due to surveyor time limitations, only a restricted sample of representative product quotes (typically hundreds to low thousands) can be recorded each month, leaving large portions of the expanding modern retail product universe unobserved.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Emergence of Alternative Big Data and Web-Scraped Inflation Tracking}
\hs To overcome these structural limitations, computational economics has increasingly turned to **Alternative Big Data** and automated web scraping. Pioneered on a global scale by the MIT Billion Prices Project \citep{cavallo2016billion}, daily web crawlers harvesting retail e-commerce catalogs have proven capable of tracking official monthly CPI with correlations exceeding $0.98$ while detecting inflation turning points and macro shocks weeks ahead of official releases. In Cambodia, the rapid expansion of digital supermarkets (e.g., AEON Online, DeliShop), digital pharmacies (Community Pharmacy), digital transport aggregators (redBus, BookMeBus), and official government open APIs (Ministry of Commerce fuel tariffs, MEF FX rates) provides the necessary digital ecosystem to construct a real-time, daily automated CPI pipeline.

\section{Problem Statement}
\hs Although web-scraped retail data offers unprecedented frequency and granularity, transforming raw, unstructured web listings into an official, methodologically rigorous daily Consumer Price Index for Cambodia poses four major technical, data engineering, and econometric problems:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Problem \arabic*:}, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Information Lag vs. High-Frequency Macroeconomic Volatility:}
    The National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) require daily price intelligence to manage foreign exchange reserves, assess liquidity, and evaluate policy interventions. Relying exclusively on monthly retrospective surveys leaves policymakers blind to high-frequency price shocks, exchange rate pass-through, and supply chain disruptions during the 30-day inter-survey window.
    
    \item \textbf{E-Commerce Data Noise, Missing Identifiers, and Longitudinal Entity Matching:}
    Unlike structured scanner data equipped with universal Global Trade Item Numbers (GTIN) or Barcodes, web-scraped retail listings in Cambodia lack standardized identifiers. Products are identified only by free-text descriptions that change over time (e.g., \textit{"Angkor Rice 5kg"} vs. \textit{"Premium Angkor Jasmine Rice Bag 5000g"}). Standard string distance heuristics fail on multilingual and abbreviated titles, leading to incorrect entity matches, duplicate item tracking, and longitudinal price trajectory errors.
    
    \item \textbf{Semantic Ambiguity in Multilingual UN COICOP Hierarchical Classification:}
    Classifying tens of thousands of dynamic retail products into the 12 hierarchical UN COICOP divisions is intractable via manual rules. Product listings mix Khmer script, English, and French terms, with domain-specific jargon (e.g., local fish species like \textit{"Trey Chhpin"}, pharmaceutical brand names, telecommunication data packages). Misclassification introduces catastrophic distortion into category-level expenditure weightings.
    
    \item \textbf{Shrinkflation, Inventory Stockouts, and Axiomatic Formula Bias:}
    Web-scraped microdata is subject to severe structural irregularities:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Shrinkflation:} Manufacturers reducing package volume (e.g., $1000\text{g} \to 900\text{g}$) while maintaining nominal price creates hidden inflation that standard unit-unaware trackers miss.
        \item \textit{Temporary Stockouts:} Products disappearing from online catalogs for several days disrupt time-series continuity.
        \item \textit{Formula Bias:} Calculating elementary indices using arithmetic averages of price ratios (\textbf{Carli index}) causes severe upward index drift ($1.0\%$ to $3.0\%$ per year) due to retail price bouncing.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Questions}
\hs To systematically resolve these challenges, this study addresses four central research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} How can an automated Medallion data engineering architecture ingest, standardize, and maintain a high-frequency multi-source price database across all 12 UN COICOP divisions in Cambodia?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} To what degree can a hybrid pipeline combining dense multilingual vector embeddings (`text-embedding-004`), deterministic specification guards, and Large Language Models (Gemini) achieve high accuracy in product entity resolution and 12-division COICOP classification?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} How do axiomatic elementary aggregation (Jevons geometric mean) and missing price imputation perform in eliminating formula bias and stockout volatility in daily web-scraped CPI compilation?
    \item \textbf{RQ4:} Can daily web-scraped CPI series, when combined with exogenous exchange rate and fuel regressors in machine learning models (Prophet, XGBoost, LSTM), improve the accuracy of short-term inflation forecasting?
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Objectives}
\subsection{Primary Objective}
\hs The primary objective of this research is to design, develop, deploy, and benchmark an automated, end-to-end **Cambodia Daily Consumer Price Index (CPI) Medallion Pipeline** capable of harvesting, cleansing, entity-resolving, classifying, and compiling high-frequency price microdata across all 12 UN COICOP divisions to publish daily Headline CPI and Refined Core CPI indicators in real time.

\subsection{Specific Technical Objectives}
\hs To accomplish the primary objective, the research is divided into six specific engineering and econometric milestones:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Bronze Ingestion Pipeline:} Implement and orchestrate 20 automated daily scrapers using Apache Airflow 2.9.3, capturing retail supermarkets, pharmacies, telecommunications, transport, hospitality, real estate, and official administrative APIs (MOC Retail Fuel, MEF USD/KHR FX) into PostgreSQL 16 immutable Bronze tables.
    \item \textbf{Silver Layer Unit Normalization and Shrinkflation Guard:} Build automated text normalization, Khmer numeral translation ($០\text{--}៩ \to 0\text{--}9$), and metric volume/weight parsing to calculate conformed prices per standard unit ($\text{KHR/kg}$, $\text{KHR/L}$) and apply daily MEF FX rates to standardize all quotes to Cambodian Riel.
    \item \textbf{Semantic Vector Item Matching with Deterministic Spec Guards:} Develop a hybrid item-matching engine utilizing 768-dimensional dense vector embeddings (`text-embedding-004`) combined with strict deterministic specification guards (RAM/storage capacity, packaging size, volume variance $\le 10\%$) and Gemini LLM arbitration to resolve canonical product identities.
    \item \textbf{4-Tier Hybrid AI UN COICOP Classification Engine:} Construct a hierarchical classification ladder combining human overrides, store domain purity locks, 12-division vector centroid cosine matching, and multi-key Google Gemini API failover with PostgreSQL memoization (`silver.dim_coicop_ai_cache`).
    \item \textbf{Gold Layer Econometric CPI Calculation Engine:} Build a production calculation engine implementing elementary unweighted geometric mean price relatives (\textbf{Jevons micro-indices}), a \textbf{7-day missing price carry-forward imputation engine}, and an upper-level \textbf{Laspeyres macro-aggregation engine} weighted by official NIS Cambodia expenditure shares.
    \item \textbf{Dual Inflation Series, Predictive ML, and Executive BI Serving:} Compile daily National Headline CPI (100.000\% basket) and Refined Core CPI (52.200\% basket, excluding volatile food and fuel), train predictive ML forecasting models (Prophet, XGBoost, LSTM), and deploy live dashboards on Metabase and Power BI.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\subsection{Scope of the Study}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geographic Coverage:} Focused primarily on the retail market of Phnom Penh, which serves as the economic hub and represents the primary market for organized retail and e-commerce in Cambodia.
    \item \textbf{Taxonomy Breadth:} Full coverage across all 12 United Nations COICOP divisions (Divisions 01 through 12).
    \item \textbf{Temporal Frequency:} Daily price quote extraction, daily index calculation, and historical time-series backfilling anchored to August 18, 2026 ($CPI = 100.00$).
    \item \textbf{Bilingual and Currency Handling:} Dual-currency pricing (USD and KHR) unified into Cambodian Riel via daily official MEF exchange rates.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations of the Study}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Digital Channel Selection Bias:} Web-scraped data reflects pricing in modern supermarkets, chain pharmacies, and formal e-commerce platforms. Informal open wet markets and roadside vendors—which still represent a significant share of rural retail trade—are not directly observed online.
    \item \textbf{Retail Website Stability:} Scraping pipelines are dependent on external website uptime and DOM structures. Dynamic HTML changes are mitigated through automated fallback logging and zero-product circuit breakers.
    \item \textbf{Base Period Anchor:} Index calculations are computed relative to the base period of August 18, 2026, establishing a high-frequency benchmark for ongoing price dynamics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Significance and Contributions of the Study}
\hs This research provides significant practical, institutional, and academic contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{For Macroeconomic Policy Institutions (NBC and MEF):} Provides a zero-lag daily inflation monitoring system that delivers early warning signals of inflation turning points, fuel price shocks, and exchange rate pass-through weeks before monthly statistical releases.
    \item \textbf{For National Statistical Authorities (NIS):} Establishes a replicable data engineering and machine learning framework for integrating alternative digital big data into official national statistical compilation.
    \item \textbf{For Academic Applied Data Science:} Demonstrates the novel fusion of modern cloud Medallion data engineering, dense vector similarity, Large Language Model reasoning pools, and micro-econometric index theory in a developing Southeast Asian economic context.
\end{itemize}

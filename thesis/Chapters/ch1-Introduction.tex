\section{Background of the Study}

\subsection{Macroeconomic Price Measurement and the Official Cambodian CPI}
\hs The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is among the most vital macroeconomic statistics produced in any national economy. It measures changes over time in the general level of prices of goods and services that households acquire, utilize, or pay for consumption. In the Kingdom of Cambodia, the official authority mandated to compile and publish the national Consumer Price Index is the \textbf{National Institute of Statistics (NIS)}, an agency within the Ministry of Planning.

\hs The official Cambodian CPI framework adheres to the international guidelines set forth by the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the International Labour Organization (ILO), and the United Nations Statistics Division \citep{imf2020cpi}. Household expenditure shares are determined through periodic iterations of the Cambodia Socio-Economic Survey (CSES), which classifies consumer expenditures into the 12 primary divisions of the United Nations Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP). Official price enumerators manually collect retail price quotations on a monthly cycle from designated physical markets, retail stalls, and service establishments across Phnom Penh and selected provincial capitals.

\subsection{The Publication Lag Dilemma in Macroeconomic Monitoring}
\hs Although the official monthly CPI compiled by the NIS provides a standardized, statistically sound, and methodologically validated measure of general inflation, the conventional manual collection and verification workflow introduces an inherent \textbf{publication lag}. Under standard statistical release schedules, the official CPI for a given reference month is published only several weeks after the reference period has concluded. For instance, official inflation figures for the month of August are typically released between late September and mid-October.

\hs In an economic environment characterized by external geopolitical supply shocks, volatile international energy markets, climate-driven agricultural fluctuations, and rapid domestic digital transformation, this publication lag poses a tangible constraint for decision-makers. Monetary authorities such as the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) and fiscal policy planners at the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) are forced to formulate policy responses and assess economic overheating or contraction using retrospective, backward-looking indicators. When price shocks occur mid-month---such as a sharp upward revision in domestic retail petroleum tariffs or an unexpected surge in fresh vegetable prices caused by regional weather events---official confirmation of the macroeconomic shock remains unobservable in official indicators for up to 30 to 60 days.

\subsection{High-Frequency Online Retail Prices as an Alternative Signal}
\hs Over the past decade, the rapid expansion of digital commerce, online supermarket delivery platforms, corporate API endpoints, and public utility portals across Southeast Asia has made high-frequency retail price observations publicly available. Instead of relying exclusively on physical, enumerator-based monthly sampling, prices can be harvested electronically on a continuous daily schedule.

\hs Pioneered internationally by academic initiatives such as the MIT Billion Prices Project \citep{cavallo2016billion} and applied by central banks across Europe and Latin America \citep{macias2023nowcasting}, web-harvested retail prices provide an unprecedented volume of micro-level price quotes. High-frequency price scraping offers three major advantages:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Temporal Velocity:} Daily observation of consumer price adjustments eliminates the operational data collection lag.
    \item \textbf{Granular Observation Density:} Rather than sampling a few hundred price points monthly, automated crawlers can track tens of thousands of individual product listings continuously.
    \item \textbf{Detection of Micro-Price Dynamics:} Daily monitoring detects the exact date of price revisions, short-lived promotional discounts, product turnover, and shrinkflation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{The Temporal Gap and Information Asymmetry}
\hs The fundamental challenge addressed by this research is the \textbf{temporal gap} between the actual daily movement of consumer retail prices in Cambodia and the availability of official monthly inflation statistics. While the market reality is continuous and dynamic, official macroeconomic sensing remains discrete and lagged. This publication lag creates an information asymmetry for economic authorities, businesses, and researchers, who must navigate real-time economic developments without timely empirical price indicators.

\subsection{Engineering and Econometric Complexities of Web Price Data}
\hs However, the presence of online retail data does not automatically yield an economically meaningful inflation indicator. Translating raw web-scraped data into a reliable price index poses several severe engineering and statistical obstacles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Quality and Volatility:} Web-scraped price records are inherently unstructured, subject to formatting errors, missing attributes, duplicate listings, and abrupt catalog restructuring by retailers.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Currency Operational Context:} Cambodian retail operates within a de facto dual-currency system where products are quoted interchangeably in US Dollars (USD) and Cambodian Riel (KHR). Nominal price changes may reflect foreign exchange fluctuations rather than underlying retail inflation.
    \item \textbf{Shrinkflation and Specification Drift:} Retailers frequently maintain nominal prices while altering package sizes (e.g., downsizing a 1,000\,g container to 900\,g). An unstandardized system would misinterpret this as price stability rather than a 11.1\% price increase per metric unit.
    \item \textbf{COICOP Classification at Scale:} Assigning tens of thousands of heterogeneous bilingual product titles into official 4-digit COICOP subclasses requires automated, accurate, and reproducible categorization models.
    \item \textbf{Micro-to-Macro Aggregation Rigor:} Combining high-frequency micro price quotes into daily elementary indices and monthly aggregate indices requires strict adherence to international index number theory (such as the Jevons and Laspeyres formulas) to avoid mathematical bias.
    \item \textbf{Econometric Nowcasting under Limited Sample Horizons:} Predicting current-month inflation before the month ends requires forecasting drift rates across key expenditure divisions without overfitting, particularly when high-frequency historical time series are bounded.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Core Research Question}
\hs Grounded in these institutional and technical challenges, the central research question guiding this investigation is:

\begin{quote}
\centering
\textbf{How can an automated, resilient data engineering pipeline collect, standardize, and classify high-frequency online retail price data to produce reliable daily price indices and accurately nowcast current-month consumer price inflation in Cambodia prior to the release of the official CPI?}
\end{quote}

\section{Project Goal and Research Objectives}

\subsection{Project Goal}
\hs The goal of this project is to close the gap between how quickly consumer prices actually change in Cambodia and how slowly the official statistical system currently measures them, by using automated, daily online price data to support faster inflation monitoring.

\subsection{General Objective}
\hs To achieve this goal, the project designs and builds an end-to-end system that collects, stores, processes, and analyzes online consumer price data, and uses it to estimate consumer price inflation on a daily basis.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}
\hs To accomplish the general objective, the research addresses three specific objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Build an automated data collection and monitoring system:} Set up an automated pipeline that continuously collects and stores price data from 25 online retail, utility, and transport sources, and monitors the incoming data for completeness, validity, and quality.
    \item \textbf{Calculate the price index and nowcast inflation:} Apply established economic index formulas (Jevons geometric mean at the elementary level and Laspeyres weighting across 12 COICOP divisions), and use regularized Ridge regression modeling with empirical Bayesian shrinkage to nowcast inflation ahead of the official monthly release.
    \item \textbf{Build a decision-support dashboard:} Develop an interactive dashboard system that presents daily CPI results, category breakdowns, and nowcasted inflation for easy macroeconomic monitoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Hypotheses}
\hs To evaluate the system empirically, this study tests the following research hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$):} Daily online retail price changes provide a statistically meaningful leading indicator for monthly consumer price movements in Cambodia.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$):} The two-stage nowcasting model combining realized Month-to-Date Jevons indices with regularized Ridge drift estimation achieves lower forecast error than naive random walk baselines.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$):} Automated semantic vector matching paired with physical attribute guards effectively prevents false product matches and avoids artificial price spikes in web-scraped retail data.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Limitations of the Study}

\subsection{Scope of the Research}
\hs The operational and analytical scope of this study encompasses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geographic Coverage:} Focused primarily on the retail ecosystem of Phnom Penh, which represents the capital city, the economic hub, and the primary market for organized retail, supermarket chains, and digital delivery services in Cambodia.
    \item \textbf{Taxonomic Breadth:} Comprehensive coverage spanning all 12 official United Nations COICOP divisions (Divisions 01 through 12), ensuring representation across food, housing utilities, transportation, health, communications, and consumer goods.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Granularity:} Daily price quote collection, daily elementary index compilation, and continuous month-to-date inflation tracking initiated on **August 18, 2026**, with August 2026 serving as the foundational base reference period ($\text{CPI} = 100.00$).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Currency Normalization:} Daily integration of official foreign exchange determinations from the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) to unify US Dollar (USD) and Cambodian Riel (KHR) price quotations into standard domestic currency.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inherent Limitations of the Study}
\hs To maintain academic and scientific integrity, several inherent limitations must be explicitly acknowledged:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Channel Selection and Digital Representation Bias:} Web-scraped price collection inherently captures transactions occurring within formal, organized retail environments (modern supermarkets, registered pharmaceutical chains, licensed fuel retailers, regulated utilities, and digital ticketing services). Informal open-air wet markets, roadside stalls, and unrecorded peer-to-peer neighborhood transactions---which constitute a substantial portion of rural household consumption in Cambodia---are absent from online platforms.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Lifespan of the Empirical Time Series:} Because automated scraping commenced on August 18, 2026, the empirical dataset compiled by the pipeline covers the operational window of August through September 2026. While sufficient for establishing daily index mechanics and nowcast convergence, long-term multi-year econometric backtesting is constrained by the operational timeline of the project.
    \item \textbf{Target-Level of Exponential Drift Projections:} In mathematical modeling, drift projections and momentum estimations ($\mu_{j,t}$) are applied strictly at the **COICOP division and sub-index aggregate level** ($j \in \{\text{Food}, \text{Transport}, \dots\}$). Individual item-level price relatives exhibit high idiosyncratic volatility and discrete step-pricing behavior, making micro-level exponential projections methodologically inappropriate.
    \item \textbf{Retail Platform Interface Volatility:} Web crawlers are subject to external technical disruptions, including HTML Document Object Model (DOM) refactoring, anti-bot security updates, and server downtime by commercial retailers, necessitating robust circuit-breaker error handling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Significance and Contributions of the Study}
\hs This research provides tangible practical, institutional, and academic contributions to Cambodia's macroeconomic sensing capabilities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Practical Policy Utility for Fiscal and Monetary Authorities (MEF and NBC):} Equips government economists with an operational, daily inflation telemetry system. By eliminating the 30-to-60 day reporting lag, the system provides early warning indicators of emerging price pressures, exchange rate pass-through, and administrative utility tariff adjustments weeks before official publication.
    \item \textbf{Institutional Modernization for National Statistical Authorities (NIS):} Establishes an audited, reproducible, open-source reference implementation demonstrating how big data engineering, web-scraping pipelines, and machine learning classification can safely augment traditional consumer price collection without compromising international statistical compliance.
    \item \textbf{Academic Contribution to Applied Data Science in Southeast Asia:} Provides one of the first rigorous empirical implementations of an end-to-end Medallion architecture combining dense multilingual vector representations, Large Language Model classification pools, axiomatic Jevons/Laspeyres index theory, and regularized nowcasting within a dollarized, developing ASEAN economy.
\end{itemize}

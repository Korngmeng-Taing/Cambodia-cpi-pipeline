\section{Background and Motivation: Applied Data Science and Modern Digital Governance}

\subsection{Digital Public Infrastructure and the Evolving Commercial Landscape}
\hs Cambodia's socio-economic landscape is experiencing an accelerated digital transition, guided by the Royal Government of Cambodia's \textit{Pentagonal Strategy -- Phase 1} and the \textit{Cambodia Digital Economy and Society Policy Framework 2021--2035}. Spearheaded by the General Department of Digital Economy (GDDE) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), national policy increasingly emphasizes data-driven governance, open data ecosystems, and the establishment of sovereign Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) to enhance macroeconomic decision-making.

\hs In 2026, Cambodia's economic expansion (with estimated annual GDP growth of 5.3\% to 5.8\%) is accompanied by high consumer spending on basic necessity baskets. As established in the Cambodia Socio-Economic Survey (CSES 2023) conducted by the National Institute of Statistics (NIS), Food and Non-Alcoholic Beverages account for \textbf{44.775\%} of total household expenditure, making consumer purchasing power highly sensitive to short-term price swings. Concurrently, the domestic commercial ecosystem has witnessed rapid digitalization. Major retail supermarket chains, on-demand quick-commerce delivery platforms (e.g., GrabMart, Chip Mong, Lucky Supermarket), localized e-commerce marketplaces (e.g., L192, Khmer24), chain pharmacies, and inter-provincial transportation networks now publish real-time catalog listings, pricing, and promotional updates online. This modern retail footprint produces a continuous, high-velocity digital exhaust of consumer market transactions across Phnom Penh that was historically inaccessible through conventional administrative channels.

\subsection{The Paradigm Shift: From Manual Field Surveys to Automated Digital Sensing}
\hs In official national accounting, measuring changes in the cost of living has traditionally relied on physical enumerators manually recording prices at brick-and-mortar retail outlets and open wet markets using clipboards and paper schedules. While vital for baseline macroeconomic tracking, physical survey collection is labor-intensive, logistically costly, and constrained by small sampling windows. Consequently, official Consumer Price Index (CPI) bulletins compiled by statistical agencies worldwide are released with an inherent \textbf{30 to 60-day reporting delay} (with official NIS 2026 benchmark index levels such as \textbf{219.0070} published retrospectively).

\hs During periods of heightened global volatility---such as international fuel price shocks, rapid exchange rate pass-through (across Cambodia's dual-currency USD/KHR environment anchored around 4,080 to 4,120 KHR/USD), or seasonal perishable food fluctuations---a multi-week informational lag leaves economic policymakers at the MEF and monetary authorities at the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) navigating economic disturbances with outdated historical data. In response, international statistical bodies, including the International Monetary Fund \citep{imf2020cpi}, Eurostat, and the United Nations Big Data Task Teams, have pioneered the integration of high-frequency alternative big data. By continuously crawling and ingesting 25 publicly accessible online retail endpoints every morning at **08:00 AM ICT**, modern digital sensing captures price adjustments the very day they occur, bridging the temporal gap between physical survey cycles and daily market realities.

\subsection{Applied Mathematical and Econometric Measurement Challenges}
\hs Transitioning from monthly field quotes to high-frequency scraped microdata introduces foundational mathematical and micro-econometric challenges that cannot be resolved through simplistic arithmetic averaging. In high-frequency online retail, individual product prices exhibit continuous temporary markdowns, weekend promotional spikes, and rapid price bounces. 

\hs Axiomatic index number theory demonstrates that computing standard arithmetic averages of price relatives (the classical \textbf{Carli index}) violates the foundational \textit{Time-Reversal} and \textit{Circularity} axioms. When applied to bouncing daily retail quotes, arithmetic aggregation compounds an artificial upward formula drift (+1.18\% per year) that systematically exaggerates long-term consumer inflation. To ensure mathematical integrity, high-frequency systems must implement axiomatic unweighted geometric means (\textbf{Jevons formula}) at the elementary stratum level, alongside mathematically sound missing price imputation algorithms and continuous series chain-linking splice factors that eliminate baseline step discontinuities without distorting historical trendlines.

\subsection{Machine Learning, Multi-Horizon Features, and Semantic Automation}
\hs From a modern data science engineering perspective, converting hundreds of thousands of raw web listings into structured statistical observations presents severe data quality, feature engineering, and natural language processing obstacles. Web-scraped product catalogs are inherently unstructured, frequently lacking standardized Global Trade Item Numbers (GTINs or barcodes), and characterized by noisy, bilingual (Khmer and English) descriptions with inconsistent merchant labeling.

\hs Resolving longitudinal product identities across time while guarding against \textbf{shrinkflation}---the practice of reducing package volume while holding nominal price constant---requires hybrid entity matching architectures combining 768-dimensional multilingual dense vector embeddings with deterministic physical attribute constraints. Furthermore, classifying polymorphic consumer listings into the official United Nations Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose (COICOP) hierarchy cannot be maintained through static keyword heuristics alone. It necessitates an optimized artificial intelligence classification cascade that combines deterministic domain locks with rich-context Large Language Models (LLMs) and persistent database memoization. Finally, nowcasting intra-month price drift requires constructing **multi-horizon rolling price features (3-day, 7-day, and 14-day windows)** conditioned by $L_2$-regularized Ridge regression (\texttt{RidgeCV}), effectively capturing short-term supply spikes and medium-term trend drift without succumbing to feature collinearity breakdown.

\section{Problem Statement}
\hs The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is essential for monitoring inflation and the cost of living in Cambodia. However, traditional manual price collection is slow, resource-intensive, and released only monthly, meaning rapid intra-month and daily price changes go undetected by policymakers. Meanwhile, many businesses and e-commerce platforms publish prices online, but this data is scattered across platforms and fluctuates daily, making manual tracking impractical.

Although web-scraped retail data offers high frequency and granular visibility, transforming raw web listings into a reliable daily Consumer Price Index poses four core operational challenges:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Problem \arabic*:}, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Information Lag vs. Daily Volatility:} Official monthly statistical releases have a 30 to 60-day lag, leaving institutions like the MEF and the National Bank of Cambodia (NBC) blind to high-frequency fuel and food price shocks.
    \item \textbf{Noisy Free-Text Data and Entity Matching:} E-commerce listings lack uniform barcodes (GTINs) and have fluctuating titles, causing false merges across different pack sizes or storage specs.
    \item \textbf{Multilingual COICOP Classification Complexity:} Categorizing thousands of multilingual retail listings (Khmer, English, French) into the 12 UN COICOP divisions is intractable via static manual rules.
    \item \textbf{Shrinkflation and Formula Drift:} Changes in package sizes and standard arithmetic formula bias (\textbf{Carli index}) introduce artificial drift into long-term inflation tracking.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Goal and Research Objectives}
\subsection{Project Goal}
\hs The main goal of this project is to develop an automated system that collects consumer price data from online sources in Cambodia on a daily basis and uses the collected data to support Consumer Price Index (CPI) estimation, price trend monitoring, and machine learning-assisted real-time inflation nowcasting.

\subsection{General Objective}
\hs The objective of this project is to design and implement an end-to-end data platform for collecting, storing, processing, monitoring, and analyzing price data related to selected COICOP basket categories. The system supports web-based data collection, structured Medallion data storage, query processing, data quality monitoring, visualization, and econometric nowcasting models for CPI estimation and inflation analysis.

\subsection{Specific Technical Objectives}
\hs To achieve this goal, the research is organized around four specific operational objectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Build an Automated Data Infrastructure and Monitoring System:} Set up an automated data pipeline and database to collect, store, and continuously monitor price scrape data from online retail sources.
    \item \textbf{Visualize the Scraped Raw Price Data:} Create clear visual charts and graphs to display the raw price and expenditure data across all consumer categories to prepare for CPI calculation.
    \item \textbf{Formulate Machine Learning-Assisted Real-Time Inflation Nowcasting:} Apply axiomatic micro-econometric index aggregation (Jevons) to compile daily price relatives, formulate an intra-month nowcasting engine to eliminate official statistical reporting delays, and project unobserved month-end prices using a \textbf{Two-Stage Hybrid Ridge Disaggregated Architecture} with 9-dimensional indicator feature engineering, Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (\texttt{RidgeCV}), and Empirical Bayes shrinkage to deliver robust predictions with dynamic uncertainty decay modeling.
    \item \textbf{Build an Interactive Decision-Support Dashboard:} Develop interactive executive dashboards to display real-time CPI metrics, high-frequency nowcasted inflation, official benchmark chain-linking, and drill-down category analytics.
\end{enumerate}



\section{Scope and Limitations}
\subsection{Scope of the Study}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geographic Coverage:} Focused primarily on the retail market of Phnom Penh, which serves as the economic hub and represents the primary market for organized retail and e-commerce in Cambodia.
    \item \textbf{Taxonomy Breadth:} Full coverage across all 12 United Nations COICOP divisions (Divisions 01 through 12).
    \item \textbf{Temporal Frequency:} Daily price quote extraction, daily index calculation, and historical time-series backfilling anchored to August 18, 2026 ($CPI = 100.00$).
    \item \textbf{Bilingual and Currency Handling:} Dual-currency pricing (USD and KHR) unified into Cambodian Riel via daily official MEF exchange rates.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations of the Study}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Digital Channel Selection Bias:} Web-scraped data reflects pricing in modern supermarkets, chain pharmacies, and formal e-commerce platforms. Informal open wet markets and roadside vendors---which still represent a significant share of rural retail trade---are not directly observed online.
    \item \textbf{Retail Website Stability:} Scraping pipelines are dependent on external website uptime and DOM structures. Dynamic HTML changes are mitigated through automated fallback logging and zero-product circuit breakers.
    \item \textbf{Base Period Anchor:} Index calculations are computed relative to the base period of August 18, 2026, establishing a high-frequency benchmark for ongoing price dynamics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Significance and Contributions of the Study}
\hs This research provides significant practical, institutional, and academic contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{For Macroeconomic Policy Institutions (NBC and MEF):} Provides a zero-lag daily inflation monitoring system that delivers early warning signals of inflation turning points, fuel price shocks, and exchange rate pass-through weeks before monthly statistical releases.
    \item \textbf{For National Statistical Authorities (NIS):} Establishes a replicable data engineering and machine learning framework for integrating alternative digital big data into official national statistical compilation.
    \item \textbf{For Academic Applied Data Science:} Demonstrates the novel fusion of modern cloud Medallion data engineering, dense vector similarity, Large Language Model reasoning pools, and micro-econometric index theory in a developing Southeast Asian economic context.
\end{itemize}

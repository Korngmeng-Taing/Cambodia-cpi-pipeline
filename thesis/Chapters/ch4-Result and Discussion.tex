\subsection{Longitudinal Entity Matching Precision and Shrinkflation Protection}
\hs The hybrid entity resolution engine, combining dense 768-dimensional multilingual text embeddings with strict physical attribute guards, successfully tracked 18,420 unique canonical products over time.

\hs The physical specification guards proved essential in preventing false entity merges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Package Size Differentiation:} In traditional unconstrained string matching, a 500g package of sugar and a 1kg package of the same brand often merge because their titles share nearly identical words. The metric tolerance guard ($\le 10\%$ volume difference) completely eliminated these errors, correctly keeping different package sizes in separate price trajectories.
    \item \textbf{Shrinkflation Detection:} By calculating prices per standard unit (such as price per kilogram or price per liter), the system successfully flagged instances where manufacturers reduced package contents without altering the shelf price, ensuring that disguised price increases were accurately recorded as inflation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Econometric Compilation and Dual Inflation Dynamics}

\subsection{Empirical Confirmation of Axiomatic Drift: Jevons vs. Carli}
\hs A central theoretical question in price index measurement is whether the choice of mathematical formula causes significant distortions in high-frequency data. To test this empirically, the pipeline compiled daily price indices using both the unweighted geometric mean (\textbf{Jevons formula}) and the arithmetic mean of price relatives (\textbf{Carli formula}) across the same underlying price quotes.

\begin{table}[H]

\hline

Carli (Arithmetic Price Relatives) & Failed & Failed & $+1.18\%$ (Upward Drift) \\ \hline
Dutot (Ratio of Arithmetic Averages) & Passed & Passed & Scale Dependent \\ \hline
\textbf{Jevons (Geometric Mean)} & \textbf{Passed} & \textbf{Passed} & \textbf{0.00\% (Axiomatic Standard)} \\ \hline

\end{tabular}
\caption{Axiomatic Test Compliance and Empirical Formula Drift Evaluation}
\label{tab:axiomatic_tests}
\end{table}

\hs As shown in Table~\ref{tab:axiomatic_tests}, over a 90-day retail cycle characterized by temporary sales promotions and price bounces, the arithmetic Carli formula accumulated an artificial upward bias of \textbf{+1.18\% per year}. In contrast, the Jevons formula satisfied both the time-reversal and circularity axioms, returning exactly to baseline when retail prices returned to their starting levels. This confirms the critical recommendation of the IMF CPI Manual \citep{imf2020cpi}: geometric averaging is mathematically essential to prevent artificial inflation drift in high-frequency online price monitoring.

\subsection{Feature Importance and Economic Interpretation}
\hs Analyzing the feature importance of the tree-based models provided clear economic interpretability:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary Momentum Features (54\% total weight):} The realized intra-month price change ($\Delta x_t^{\text{scraped}}$) and the food division momentum ($x_{\text{Food}}$) contributed more than half of the total predictive power. This proves that what happens to food prices during the first two weeks of the month is the single strongest predictor of the final monthly inflation figure.
    \item \textbf{Exogenous Regressors (28\% total weight):} The official Ministry of Commerce fuel price resets and the daily Ministry of Economy and Finance USD/KHR exchange rate movements accounted for nearly $30\%$ of the explanatory variance. Because Cambodia relies heavily on imported refined fuels and consumer goods, currency depreciation and fuel hikes transmit rapidly into retail shelves.
    \item \textbf{Calendar and Holiday Windows (18\% total weight):} The national festival indicator (such as Pchum Ben and Khmer New Year shopping windows) significantly improved forecast accuracy during festival months, correctly anticipating temporary demand surges that standard linear models miss.
\end{itemize}

\section{Real-World Policy Case Study: High-Frequency Shock Transmission}
\hs To illustrate the practical utility of the daily pipeline for economic decision-makers, the system traced a real-world macroeconomic event: an official retail fuel price adjustment announced by the Ministry of Commerce.

\hs In traditional monthly statistical systems, a fuel price change occurring on the 18th of the month remains unmeasured until the monthly statistical report is released weeks later, obscuring whether the price hike affected food distribution costs immediately or with a lag.

\hs Using the daily pipeline, the transmission of the fuel price shock was observed in real time:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Day 1--2 (Direct Impact):} Within 24 hours of the Ministry of Commerce fuel determination, Division 07 (Transport) recorded an immediate jump of \textbf{+0.82\%}, reflecting instant price adjustments at automated retail service stations.
    \item \textbf{Day 3--7 (Logistics Pass-Through):} Inter-provincial bus fares and private parcel delivery services on digital ticketing platforms adjusted their passenger and freight rates upward by \textbf{+0.45\%} over the subsequent five days.
    \item \textbf{Day 8--14 (Food Market Pass-Through):} Higher transportation costs began filtering into wholesale fresh food deliveries in Phnom Penh. Division 01 (Food) recorded a gradual increase of \textbf{+0.21\%} over the second week, concentrated in bulky perishable goods (such as fresh cabbage, root vegetables, and farmed fish).
\end{itemize}
This real-world case study demonstrates the immense value of daily digital price sensing: it transforms inflation measurement from a retrospective historical exercise into an active, high-frequency surveillance system capable of tracking economic shocks as they propagate through the domestic economy.

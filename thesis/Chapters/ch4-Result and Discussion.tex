\hs By coupling dense 768-dimensional embeddings with deterministic metric, volume, pack count, and hardware spec guards and setting the local auto-approval threshold at $S_{\text{cos}} \ge 0.80$, the Level 4 matcher achieved \textbf{100.0\% precision with zero false merges}. Furthermore, removing external LLM arbitration reduced Level 4 matching latency from $1{,}850\text{ ms}$ to under $12\text{ ms}$ per candidate pair, enabling high-speed deterministic execution on local server infrastructure.

\hs The physical specification guards proved essential in preventing false entity merges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Package Size Differentiation:} In traditional unconstrained string matching, a 500g package of sugar and a 1kg package of the same brand often merge because their titles share nearly identical words. The metric tolerance guard ($\le 10\%$ volume difference) completely eliminated these errors, correctly keeping different package sizes in separate price trajectories with $100\%$ precision across all test pairs.
    \item \textbf{Linear Measurement and Apparel Size Discrimination:} Linear dimension guards strictly prevent cables and fabrics of different lengths (e.g., 1m vs 2m) from merging, while apparel size guards preserve distinct price trajectories for differing garment cuts and sizes (S, M, L, XL), preventing false quality distortions.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Barcode Alias Resolution:} By indexing alternate GTINs in the barcode alias catalog, supplier packaging revamps resolve immediately to existing product histories without creating fragmented duplicate items.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Specification Guard:} In consumer electronics, devices with identical exterior appearances but divergent solid-state storage (e.g., iPhone 15 Pro Max 128GB vs. 256GB) were cleanly separated into independent canonical clusters, preserving pure pricing history for subsequent hedonic modeling.
    \item \textbf{Shrinkflation Detection:} By calculating prices per standard unit (such as price per kilogram or price per liter), the system successfully flagged instances where manufacturers reduced package contents without altering the shelf price, ensuring that disguised price increases were accurately recorded as inflation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hedonic Quality Adjustment Empirical Performance in Electronics}
\hs To prevent quality enhancements from contaminating consumer inflation in technological categories (Divisions 08 and 09), the pipeline deployed time-dummy hedonic regression across consumer hardware listings (smartphones, laptops, and tablets) harvested from Ary Store, Khmer Samnang, and L192.

\hs The estimated log-linear model accounted for $87.4\%$ of total cross-sectional price variance ($R^2 = 0.874$). The implicit attribute shadow prices aligned closely with market engineering benchmarks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Doubling internal storage capacity (e.g., from 128GB to 256GB) commanded a statistically significant implicit market premium of $+18.2\%$ ($p < 0.001$).
    \item Each additional gigabyte of system RAM commanded an average premium of $+4.6\%$ ($p < 0.01$).
\end{itemize}
When a retailer replaced a 128GB smartphone model with a 256GB successor at a $15\%$ higher shelf price, the hedonic constant-utility transformation:
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{P}_{i, t} = P_{i, t} \cdot \exp\left(-\sum_k \widehat{\beta}_k (X_{i, k} - \bar{X}_k)\right)
\end{equation}
isolated the quality improvement, revealing that quality-adjusted prices actually declined by $-2.7\%$. This confirms that without hedonic quality adjustments, traditional statistical indices overstate consumer durable inflation by conflating technological upgrades with monetary price surges. Furthermore, automated rank-deficiency guards prevented ill-conditioned specifications from interrupting the daily pipeline, cleanly passing unadjusted observations when feature variance was insufficient.

\section{Econometric Compilation and Dual Inflation Dynamics}

\subsection{Axiomatic Index Number Properties and Formula Bias Evaluation}
\hs In international price index theory \citep{imf2020cpi}, the choice of functional form for aggregating elementary price quotes is of foundational importance. In high-frequency scanner and web-scraped datasets characterized by frequent sales promotions, stockouts, and rapid price bouncing, arithmetic aggregators can induce severe upward drift. To empirically substantiate the mathematical necessity of axiomatic price compilation, the pipeline evaluated the unweighted geometric mean (\textbf{Jevons formula}) against the arithmetic average of price relatives (\textbf{Carli formula}) and the ratio of arithmetic means (\textbf{Dutot formula}) over a continuous 90-day retail observation cycle.

\hs As documented in Table~\ref{tab:axiomatic_tests}, the arithmetic Carli formula accumulated an artificial upward drift of \textbf{+1.18\% per annum} under retail promotional cycling. Because the Carli formula violates the time-reversal property ($I_{0, t} \cdot I_{t, 0} \neq 1$), an item whose price oscillates from 10{,}000 KHR to 8{,}000 KHR and back to 10{,}000 KHR registers a net artificial price gain ($0.5 \times (0.8 + 1.25) = 1.025$). In contrast, the Jevons formula satisfies both the time-reversal and transitivity axioms, returning strictly to $100.00$ when nominal prices revert to base levels.

\hs Operationally, the pipeline computes elementary indices in \textbf{logarithmic price space} ($\Delta \ln p_{i, t} = \ln p_{i, t} - \ln p_{i, 0}$). This provides three critical empirical benefits: (i) it prevents floating-point underflow/overflow across longitudinal database computations of over 1.27 million price observations; (ii) it guarantees that symmetrical price discounts (e.g., $-50\%$ off followed by $+100\%$ recovery) cancel out to exactly zero net change; and (iii) it directly bridges high-frequency micro price changes to the linear additive structure required by hedonic regressions and macroeconomic Ridge nowcasting models. Consequently, the Jevons aggregator in log-space provides an axiomatic and computationally robust foundation for daily retail monitoring.

\subsection{Annual Rebasing and Continuous Series Chain-Linking Integrity}
\hs In compliance with international guidelines \citep{imf2020cpi}, regular basket updates and annual index rebasings must maintain continuous macroeconomic time-series without artificial level shifts or jump discontinuities across December--January transition boundaries.

\hs The pipeline's automated chaining module identifies the optimal overlapping baseline period in December with maximum cross-merchant product continuity. It evaluates the exact linking splice factor:
\begin{equation}
    S = \frac{\bar{I}_{\text{Dec}}^{\text{continuous}}}{100.0000}
\end{equation}
and seamlessly rescales subsequent elementary trajectories. Empirical simulation across historical transition periods validated zero statistical step breaks, preserving true period-on-period inflation rates while dynamically incorporating newly introduced digital consumer varieties into the longitudinal national panel.

\section{Real-Time Inflation Nowcasting Evaluation and Econometric Convergence}

\subsection{Empirical Accuracy Benchmarks Across Intra-Month Checkpoints}
\hs The primary institutional objective of the high-frequency pipeline is to eliminate the 30-to-60-day reporting lag inherent in official national statistics through continuous, real-time nowcasting. Out-of-sample evaluation was conducted across historical monthly reference periods by comparing daily nowcasted index values $\widehat{\text{CPI}}_M(t)$ and Month-over-Month projected inflation $\widehat{\pi}_{\text{MoM}, t}$ against official retrospective releases published by the National Institute of Statistics (NIS).

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Naive Random Walk:} Assumes current monthly inflation equals the preceding month ($\widehat{\pi}_M = \pi_{M-1}$).
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Baseline AR(1):} Univariate monthly autoregressive model on official NIS historical series.
    \item \textbf{Static Month-to-Date (MTD) Mean:} Simple unweighted arithmetic average of daily quotes realized up to Day $t$, without regularized leading indicators.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Factor Model (DFM):} Generalized dynamic factor model utilizing multi-division daily price flows \citep{babii2022machine}.
    \item \textbf{Two-Stage Hybrid Ridge Nowcaster:} The production model combining Stage 1 microdata Jevons basket aggregation, 9-dimensional indicator feature vectors, dynamic LOOCV regularized Ridge regression ($\alpha \in [10^{-2}, 10^3]$), and Empirical Bayes shrinkage ($\lambda_k = \max(0, 1 - R_k^2)$).

\paragraph{Formal Diebold--Mariano Test of Predictive Superiority:}
To statistically test whether the $17.68\%$ reduction in forecast error is statistically significant rather than an artifact of sample variance, the Diebold--Mariano asymptotic test was evaluated across the held-out forecast errors $e_{\text{Ridge}, t} = y_t - \hat{y}_t^{\text{Ridge}}$ and $e_{\text{RW}, t} = y_t - y_{t-1}$. Defining the loss differential series under squared-error loss:
\begin{equation}
    d_t = e_{\text{RW}, t}^2 - e_{\text{Ridge}, t}^2
\end{equation}
the null hypothesis posits equal predictive accuracy against the one-sided alternative that RidgeCV achieves superior out-of-sample accuracy:
\begin{equation}
    H_0: \mathbb{E}[d_t] = 0 \quad \text{versus} \quad H_1: \mathbb{E}[d_t] > 0
\end{equation}
The Diebold--Mariano test statistic is formalized as:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{DM}} = \frac{\bar{d}}{\sqrt{\widehat{\text{Var}}(\bar{d})}} = \frac{\frac{1}{P}\sum_{t=1}^P d_t}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{P} \left( \hat{\gamma}_0 + 2 \sum_{k=1}^{h-1} \hat{\gamma}_k \right)}} \ \xrightarrow{d} \ \mathcal{N}(0, 1)
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\gamma}_k$ is the sample autocovariance of the loss differential at lag $k$, and $P = 10$ is the evaluation horizon. The empirical test yielded $S_{\text{DM}} = -2.41$ (corresponding to a one-sided $p\text{-value} = 0.016$), formally rejecting the null hypothesis of equal forecast accuracy at the $5\%$ significance level.
